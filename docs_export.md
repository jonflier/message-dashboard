Natural Born Follower
@ Wednesday Night Youth Class (5/24/23)

| Purpose | Method |
| Edification | Topical |
| Thesis Statement |
| Every Christian is called to lead others, but effective leadership begins with faithfully following and serving Christ. |

Now, to start off, by a show of hands who has heard the term “natural born leader?”
Initially, I had thought that term just meant that someone was excellent at leading by their nature- and to the world I suppose it does. But when we look at that term as followers of God, we can then see how we are all called to be leaders.
It isn’t what position you have in your church… school… or even life. As Christians, we are constantly leading others. For many of us, if not all, there are others that look up to us. They may be our age- they may be younger- possibly even older- and yet, can still view us in the same light. I’d like for the light that people view me with, to be the same light that leads others to Christ.
In fact, John 8:12 reads-
“Then spake Jesus again unto them, saying, I am the light of the world: he that followeth me shall not walk in darkness, but shall have the light of life.” John 8:12 KJV
We are leading by example, by living Christian lives and walking in the world, others will begin to follow whether we intended for them to or not.
We are Ambassadors for Christ, according to 1 Corinthians 5:20, so the adversary is gonna do everything he can to make us look bad. We have the responsibility to show others the love that Christ has shown for us since creation, and even prior to that.
We as humans, we were made to have a relationship with God, we draw near to Him just as we were created to. When we’re filled with His Spirit and living for Him, others will draw closer to us, in order to further seek the Lord.
But before we can excel at being leaders, we must first be followers- servants to others.
In the Book of John, chapter 13, Jesus, God, our Creator- manifested in the flesh- washes the disciples feet. Jesus then tells them that if He, their master, was to serve them, they too should go out and serve others. Jesus proves that absolutely no one is too good for servitude.
Another verse about serving others-
“For, brethren, ye have been called unto liberty; only use not liberty for an occasion to the flesh, but by love serve one another.” Galatians 5:13 KJV
In Ephesians 5, verses 1-2 it reads,
“Be ye therefore followers of God, as dear children; and walk in love, as Christ also hath loved us, and hath given himself for us an offering and a sacrifice to God for a sweetsmelling savour.” Ephesians 5:1-2 KJV
The Apostle Paul was a great leader- does anyone know why that is? It is my belief that the reason Paul was such a great leader was because he was following God. When you’re following someone who knows where they’re going, you look like you know where you’re going- even when you don’t. Not to say Paul didn’t know where he was going, but we humans often don’t. So we should put our faith in God and follow Him- because God is omniscient, He is all-knowing.
This is stated in 1 John 3:20, it reads-
“For if our heart condemn us, God is greater than our heart, and knoweth all things.” 1 John 3:20 KJV
Paul writes in 1 Corinthians 11 verse 1,
“Be ye followers of me, even as I also am of Christ.” 1 Corinthians 11:1 KJV
So Paul tells people to be followers of him because he himself is a follower of God, therefore telling people to follow God.
(i.e. Let’s say Zach goes somewhere, Dagon follows him, and I follow Dagon, in reality, I’m really following Zach. Which is great because I’m awful with geography and Zack seems like he’d be a geographer and could help me find where to go. Well God is our geographer, He leads us to the path He has designed for us- which is always the path most beneficial for us.)
You should be familiar with the verse after our Youth Revival- does anyone remember Matthew 16:24? (Would anyone like to recite it and/or read it?)
“Then said Jesus unto his disciples, If any man will come after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross, and follow me.” Matthew 16:24 KJV
So, let me end off with this- while we are born leaders, we must too be followers of Christ. We must allow ourselves to be natural born followers, natural born servants, and God will guide us through the rest. We just have to obey His will.

## God’s Will For Your Life
**Source:** Google Doc | **Date:** 08.07.23

God’s Will For Your Life
@ Project 7 SU23 Wrapup [Keynote] (8/7/23)

| Purpose | Method |
| Edification | Topical |
| Thesis Statement |
| Believers do not need to know every detail of God's plan; they need only trust His perfect will, seek His guidance, and faithfully obey Him one step at a time. |

Genesis
39 Old, 27 New, and 66 Total
Moses wrote the Pentateuch
True! Adam and Eve did have three sons, the third son’s name is the answer to the next question.
Seth was the third son of Adam and Eve
Methuselah was the oldest person in the Bible, living to be 960 years old. Adam lived to be 930, so did Noah, and Moses died at 120.
Methuselah’s grandson is Noah, his great grandson is Ham, his great-great grandson is Cush, and his great-great-great grandson is Nimrod, who was responsible for building the Tower of Babel
Israel had twelve sons, which is where we get the twelve tribes, but he also is accounted for having a daughter named Dinah.
The ninth commandament is to not bear false witness, or to not lie. Do not kill is the sixth, do not steal is the eighth, and do not covet is the tenth.
Hezekiah is another spelling for Ezekias.
Jesus had twelve original Apostles.
False! Jesus was a Nazarene being from Nazareth but not a Nazarite like we read about Samson being.
James and John are named the “Sons of Thunder” in Mark 3:17.
John the Baptist, who was also Jesus’ cousin, baptized Him.
After Judas’ betrayal, Matthias became the thirteenth Apostle.
Joseph Caiaphas was the high priest that put Jesus on trial.
Jesus was buried in the tomb of Joseph of Arimathea.
Luke wrote the Book of Acts to Theophilus.
Stephen was the first martyr, of whom Saul helped stone before becoming Paul.
1 Corinthians Ch. 13 is known as the “Love Chapter” because it teaches about love and charity.
To be honest, I initially struggled to put together this lesson.
I pondered, or more so, brooded- over what I was going to teach.
I had a few past lesson drafts, and new lesson drafts and I wasn’t sure what was going to come of this.
In my prayers, I felt myself almost begging- if not begging- that God would show me His will.
That He would just show me what I was supposed to teach about.
This happened a couple of times but I learned that there wasn’t a need to beg for God’s Will at all, there’s only the need to follow it.
Don’t get me wrong, it’s perfectly okay to ask for God’s direction and guidance for your life, but I was stressing that I wasn’t a part of that direction entirely.
However, I realized that if we’re living for God and His Truth, following and obeying Him, then there shouldn’t be a problem with us not being in His Will.
Plus I didn’t need to know His entire Will to teach this lesson, I just wanted to be sure that I was in His Will.
So I’m here to tell you that you don’t have to be uncertain about God’s plan for you.
God has a plan, it’s a perfect plan, with the best outcome for your life, whether it always seems that way or not.
In Jeremiah 29:11 (in the King James Version) we read,
“For I know the thoughts that I think toward you, saith the LORD, thoughts of peace, and not of evil, to give you an expected end.” Jeremiah 29:11
Now, the Old Testament was originally written in Hebrew, and the original word for “thoughts” as used in this verse is maḥăšāḇâ (makh-ash-aw-baw’).
This word also means plan or purpose- therefore, God is telling us that he knows His plans for us and that He has a purpose for our lives.
There’s a quote that says, “We may not know what tomorrow holds, but we do know who holds tomorrow,” and His name is Jesus Christ.
So no matter what happens in our lives, just know that we don’t have to know His plan, we only have to be faithful and aware that He knows our plan.
While writing this, I still had my doubts, the doubts coming from my carnality- my flesh- things got in the way, but God’s Word teaches about perseverance.
The Bible tells us all the time to be steadfast or to persevere.
Paul puts it this way in 1 Corinthians 15:58, he says-
“Therefore, my beloved brethren, be ye stedfast, unmoveable, always abounding in the work of the Lord, forasmuch as ye know that your labour is not in vain in the Lord.”
When I didn’t know what to do about this lesson, if it was going to sound good, or if this was what I was meant to bring to you guys today, all I could do was move forward.
I didn’t have time to worry- we don’t have time to worry in life, it’s too short, which is why I’m glad God’s Word teaches us to have a sound mind.
In 2 Timothy 1:7 it says,
“For God hath not given us the spirit of fear; but of power, and of love, and of a sound mind.”
God gives us His Spirit, allowing us to have a sound mind.
However, it is our choice to accept His Spirit and all that it brings.
I wasn’t accepting the ability to have a sound mind when I was worrying instead of putting my faith in God and His Plan.
So, if you truly believe that you’re in God’s Will, then there’s no harm in praying about it anyway.
And if you realize that you aren’t in His will, then praying about it is a must.
But either way, I encourage all of us, myself included, to pray.
Pray for God’s direction, admit we cannot direct ourselves, surrender to God, ask Him to take control of our lives, and obey His Word.
Thank you…

Back to School
@ Wednesday Night Youth Service (8/23/23)

| Purpose | Method |
| Edification | Topical |
| Thesis Statement |
| Followers of Christ must reject every false idol, practice true repentance by turning from and fleeing sin, and pursue a life wholly devoted to God. |

Introduction
I want to thank Tyler and Sova for allowing me to speak to you all on what I believe the Lord has put on my heart.
I know that most of us have been in school for a couple of weeks now, and I do hope it’s going well for everybody. I just wanted to encourage everybody not to get too comfortable by being back in school, and to remain sensitive to God and everything He did through you over the past Summer.
I’m well aware it’s easy to go back into old tendencies or habits (being in a place that’s so familiar with those same things). It’s easy to go back to old relationships that probably aren’t all that good for your spirit. To go with the routine of everyone else, to want to fit in and act, or even dress, the way that some of your peers do. But I just feel the need to say that’s not God’s Will for you.
God wants you to continue on your journey of becoming your best self. He wants you to keep improving as you go through this school year, not stopping just as you leave Summer behind. He wants you to lead others to Him- that’s what we as Christians are called to do, we’re meant to bring people to God so He can do the rest. We’re meant to teach people about the power of Jesus in their lives, the baptism of His name, and the filling of His Spirit.
False Idols
When we start to serve other things- secular things- things not of God, I believe we upset the Lord. Don’t get me wrong, He loves us, but just like a parent, we can make Him angry or sad with our behavior. An example of this is found in Exodus 32, verses 9-10. For some context, the people of Israel have already been set out of slavery from Egypt through the power of God. Despite this, they feel the need to serve a false idol, a golden calf. I’ll start reading…
“And the LORD said unto Moses, I have seen this people, and, behold, it is a stiffnecked people: now therefore let me alone, that my wrath may wax hot against them, and that I may consume them: and I will make of thee a great nation.” Exodus 32:9-10 KJV
The Lord is angry when we serve false idols, and, just like the people of Israel, we should know better than that. Sometimes, however, we don’t even realize we’re serving something instead of God. Luckily, the Lord is merciful- skipping down to verse 14…
“And the LORD repented of the evil which he thought to do unto his people.” Exodus 32:14 KJV
The Lord repented, He changed the direction from pain He was going to inflict on those who sinned against Him, to mercy and compassion.
So if we find ourselves serving a false idol, or just sinning as a whole, we also need to repent. If God repented- if He changed his direction- both in His divine nature and in His human nature as Jesus Christ, then we should too.
Repentance
Most of us should know what repentance is. Can anyone tell me?
But did you know that repentance isn’t only about turning 180º from our sins, it’s also about fleeing from them too.
“Enter not into the path of the wicked, And go not in the way of evil men. Avoid it, pass not by it, Turn from it, and pass away.” Proverbs 4:14-15 KJV
This says to not only turn, but also pass away from sin.
“Flee also youthful lusts: but follow righteousness, faith, charity, peace, with them that call on the Lord out of a pure heart.” 2 Timothy 2:22 KJV
This says to flee from sinful desires, running toward the other direction, which contains everything holy.
Don’t think that running from your sins shows fear- it actually shows the opposite. Those who run from the temptation of sin are braver than those who don’t.
For those of you who were able to attend, at NAYC, one pastor mentioned the Bible story of Eli and his wicked sons, Hophni and Phinehas. Eli was a holy man, and it can be assumed that he was a man who knew how to turn away from sin. But what he didn’t know was how to turn sin away from him. He didn’t flee from it or flee it from him and his sons. I encourage you to read that story when you get the chance, but spoiler alert, the price of sin is death, and Eli’s sons paid that price. No one wants you to pay the price of sin, Jesus sure didn’t, which is why He shed His innocent blood on the Cross, and He died for you. I’m wrapping up my lesson, but I ask that we find the time to pray, to repent for the things in our life that we realize don’t belong, and ask God for the same mercy He’s given time and time again.

The Race to Heaven
@ Sunday Night Service [Fiery Five] (10/8/23)

| Purpose | Method |
| Edification | Topical |
| Thesis Statement |
| The Christian life is a race that requires perseverance, holiness, and unity, and those who faithfully stay on God's path and finish the race will receive the reward of eternal life. |

Introduction
I firstly want to thank Pastor and Sister Fayth for allowing me this opportunity. It is quite an honor to speak to you all here tonight.
Before I do so, though, I want to give some context. This year has been my first year running cross-country, not only in high school but ever. I am a sprinter in track, but I wanted to push myself and partake in this sport before I graduated.
With that being said, I want to speak to you all about the advice that I have received and learned while running cross country and make comparisons and applications to biblical truths.
I would like to make simple comparisons at the start so that it will be easier to convey my message later.
Being a runner is a lifestyle, just like being a Christian is a lifestyle. Some people were born runners; maybe they are a generational runner, and they knew what was expected of them. But to others, it is scary; all these people around them seem like they know everything about it, making it hard to become one and live for… well, as a Christian, it may be hard to live for God without the helping hand of someone familiar with the lifestyle. That is why it is important to note that we as Christians are a team; it is our job to recruit. Now, we cannot recruit at Cumberland High School, but I am thankful that we can recruit others as a church! We have our Coach (point up)… and we have His instruction (pick up Bible)...
	Let us talk about what we need before the race.

Before the Race
Before you start a race, there are two things that you need to have in check. You can adjust them both during the race, but having them figured out prior will help you to become more effective later.
That first thing is your mentality, it is the way you think, the thoughts you have toward your Coach and your teammates, all of that matters. Did you know that every time we have a negative thought, whether it be towards ourselves or others, we are actually worshiping demons? Allow me to read to you from 1 Corinthians, chapter 10, verse 20, it says- 
“But I say, that the things which the Gentiles sacrifice, they sacrifice to devils, and not to God: and I would not that ye should have fellowship with devils.” 1 Corinthians 10:20
Okay, so you are probably asking yourself, How are thoughts of negativity praising demons? What could we possibly be sacrificing? Well, I will tell you, for starters, we are sacrificing all of the Fruits of the Spirit- one cannot bear love and hate at the same time, just like one cannot bear gentleness and brutality simultaneously. The Bible tells us that the LORD has given us a spirit of freedom and of a sound mind. We cannot have a sound mind when we allow negativity into it. So, without getting our mentality in check, we will often find ourselves praising and giving in to the things of this world.
The second thing you must have in check is your form. All types of forms come with practice; most of us know the saying that practice makes permanent. The more I run, the better I become at the task at hand. The same works as Christians; the more that we put forth and demonstrate spiritual values, the easier it is for us to live for God. Now, hear me, I did not say it would be easy, but rather easier… If things like running and living for God were easy, would not everybody be doing them? That is the harsh truth: that things are going to be hard, but do not worry, we are going to talk about perseverance here soon.
Now we move on to what we can do during the race.

During the Race
	My cross country coach teaches about these things called negative splits- it is this idea that when you are running a race, each mile is supposed to be faster than the last, which I myself have not yet figured out how to do. While this idea is realistic when it comes to running, it is not quite as realistic when it comes to our walk with God. Now, what do I mean by that? You will struggle in life; we are human, and that is what we do, whether we want to or not. But my not getting negative splits in a race does not make me a bad runner, just like it does not make any of us “bad” Christians. Struggling is inevitable, but it does not mean that you are doing something wrong.
Another thing that I learned was that running a race will make you sore and exhausted (I mean, who could have guessed that, right?). But if you think about it, life does the same thing… Life is like a race that seemingly lasts forever, except that it does not. The Bible reads, in 1 Peter 4:7,
“But the end of all things is at hand: be ye therefore sober, and watch unto prayer.” 1 Peter 4:7 KJV
The end times that the Bible tells us about are closer than ever, and while this may sound obvious, it does not change how frightening it is. You don't have to watch the news to see how bad the world is; you can simply go outside and see it for yourself. However, the world looks the way it does because the enemy is frightened, even more than we are. He is scared because of how close the finish line is for all of us; that is why it is imperative to hear what the LORD has put on my heart.
Everybody has a unique course in life, and while the destination remains the same, everyone faces different obstacles that they must conquer. There will be times when we become distracted by things outside of our course.
Some of us may become distracted by other people not running the course. Maybe it is their clothing that looks appealing, but we need to realize that clothing, apart from our “uniforms,” would not protect us against the elements. It may be hard to believe, but yes, we all are wearing uniforms, ones that were designed by the Coach- God himself when he created holiness standards. Other clothes may seemingly help you move more freely when you run, or keep you cool off, but you are not going to like it when it rains- it is going to cause you to struggle to get to the finish line. No one said everybody was going to enjoy the uniform; no one said it was going to be comfortable, which is why, quite frankly, it is difficult to stick to the uniform, but we must.
It is also possible that we become distracted by what other people who are not running are doing, how they are living their lives, and it tempts us to live like them too. I do not see anyone disagreeing with me when I say that, in America and likely in most of the world, sports are a big deal to many people. When we are running, we sometimes become tempted by what is going on around us. If we see a sports game, we may want to stray off a little bit and immerse ourselves in it. Now, being tempted is not a sin, but giving in to that temptation will cause you to stumble. You may wander off the course and stumble upon some tree roots you hadn't seen, but do remember, you were not intended to be there; that is what the guardrails are for: they force you to stay on the path instead of walking off it.
There is a quote that we have all heard: “The only way we could lose this fight is if we quit.” For those who do not know, that quote was from Alex Spooner on Friday night. But I really liked what he had to say, and it made me realize that life is a race against yourself; you only lose if you quit before you finish. So while the devil may break us down during our run, he cannot destroy us- he needs us to stop.
Here is what comes after the race.

After the Race
Very rarely- not that it is impossible- but very seldom do I think to myself during a run, I feel great right now. Just like in life, there have hopefully been many times that we do feel great, but we also do not feel good a majority of this race, but we will in the end. After you run, there is a naturally occurring chemical in the brain that causes one to feel happiness. So after the race, you are feeling great, you may want to immerse yourself in an ice bath… (pick up Bible) and by that I mean immerse yourself in the Word, as advised by our Coach.

Conclusion
	Now, I have said all of that to say this: the prize at the end is not some medal to wear around your neck or plaque to hang up on the wall- it is eternal life with God! Remember how I mentioned that feeling at the end of a race, the naturally occurring chemical? Well, that is nothing compared to how great we will feel when we finish this race and see God standing before our very eyes. That is because the finish line is Heaven, and there is only one way to get there, it is the path that is straight and narrow, trodden on by few, as described in Matthew 7:14. Although it sometimes does not look like part of the course, we have to trust our Coach that it is. Brother Spooner has been prompting us to ask the question,"... "What if we stay?... How much farther do you really have to go compared to how far you have already gone? I do not know everybody’s situation, but I can promise you that if you have made it this far, then quitting is counterproductive.
More than anything, cross country has taught me to persevere, no matter how sore or tired I become; the objective is to finish the race as a team. This is the mindset we as Christians should have- finish the race- but more importantly, finish the race as a team. Pastor has taught all of us about the importance of unity, so look at the people sitting around you right now; those are your teammates. And those who are not here, the sick and the lost, those are also your teammates; either way, we must keep our team accountable during this race. The cross-country coaches always tell us to find a buddy when we are running. The race does not seem as long or as complicated when you have someone by your side. However, it is also vital that the person you are running with knows where they are going. When we try to follow people who do not know the Way, who do not know God, we find ourselves lost in the world. So let us go… and run this race toward Heaven- thank you!

We Are the Artwork of God
@ Thursday Morning FCA (12/14/23)

| Purpose | Method |
| Edification | Topical |
| Thesis Statement |
| God uses every trial and barrier in our lives to shape our character, deepen our faith, and mold us into the people He created us to be. |

Introduction
I appreciate this opportunity to speak to all of you this morning. For those who may not know me, my name is Jonathan Flier, and I have been a sprinter in track throughout high school, and I ran Cross Country this year to better prepare for my senior year of track.
I wanted to talk to you all about the barriers in life, which happen both on and off the field or court. There are several trials and tribulations that I have faced in life- some of which I am still facing. Now, if you do not mind, I am going to be transparent with you- I am going to speak to you all about some trials I have gone through, but I am only doing so to show that no matter how difficult life gets- how brutal the storm seems- there is a light on the other end- something to be gained.
Pride
During track season last year, many incoming freshmen decided to run. With the addition of these underclassmen and a few others, my typical ten-person track team jumped up to forty. To make matters worse (or at least this was my thought at the time), these underclassmen were speedy, they were athletic, and seemingly had a better mental attitude than I did. Instead of putting in maximum effort like I should have, I had this underlying sense of pride. I subconsciously thought that I deserved to run at every meet and that I would run, given it was my junior year, which led to me becoming an alternate for a couple of meets, one of them on my birthday, and the other one at sectionals. I was unable to run because I did not do what was required.
I am not expecting nor do I want pity- I am well aware and can admit that this was my fault, but I was able to learn from this incident. I have become less prideful after my teammates humbled me, and I have learned to try harder and to work as a team. I became thankful for every one of these teammates, and even if I miss out on a couple of meets, I will be thrilled to have very capable individuals in my stead.
Influence
	As I mentioned before, I ran cross country earlier this year for the first time. I quickly realized what distance running meant — I had gone from running 400 meters in track to six miles (this was 24x the distance).
There were days I struggled to maintain the energy I needed for everyday occurrences; there were occasions when I fell asleep during practice while actively running. Thankfully, it never affected me during a meet, but after the cross-country season ended, I went to the doctor to check that nothing severe was happening. So, I explained to my doctor the exhaustion I would have before eating and the combination of bouncing off the walls and feeling “chilly” after most meals. She told me they would continue the blood tests we scheduled, but believed, from what I told her, that my food habits were to blame. When she initially explained it to me, I thought she was worried I was not eating enough, so I let her know I was eating six pancakes every morning- needless to say, she was not impressed. She further informed me that I was not getting enough of what I needed to replenish my body as an athlete.
I learned that there is a reason Coach Ault gives the track kids a list of dietary suggestions at the start of their season. Or why the cross-country team would eat pasta every Thursday to better prepare for our Saturday meets. There is even a reason why both of my coaches disliked soda and energy drinks. It matters what we put into our physical bodies, and additionally, it matters what we put into our spiritual bodies. While I use the example of food, sometimes people influence us too, for better or for worse, so we should surround ourselves with a positive influence.
Loss
	This last barrier I have had to overcome has been the hardest because, quite honestly, I have not yet. My grandma passed away a couple of Sundays ago- the whole family and I were there with her when it happened. She and I were very close; I was one of her two grandsons, and I had been her “little boy” my whole life. Whenever our household was no longer safe to live in during 5th grade, she and Papaw took us in with no questions asked. I was able to live with her for a couple of months, which only brought us closer.
The last conversation I had with my grandma, when she was still responsive, was the Friday before she passed away. We were getting ready to leave, so we said our goodbyes — I did not think it would be our final goodbye. She told me that she loved me and to continue doing the good that I had been doing. So while I am still getting through this storm, I have already seen a piece of light. This memory that I will forever hold onto has and will continue to motivate me to be my best self.
Conclusion
So, as I close, listen when I say that these trials and tribulations — the overcoming of these barriers — help us to grow. It is also essential to know that Romans 12:12 says to be patient in tribulation, while continuously persevering with prayer. The LORD would not have us endure something He knew we could not handle. Genesis 2:7 tells us that the very first human, Adam, was formed as dust from the ground, and Eve later came from Adam, so no matter who you are now, you originate from the dust beneath the beasts of the earth. However, the Creator wanted us to be more, so he formed us, like clay.

A Roaring Lion
@ Wednesday Night Youth Class (4/17/24)

| Purpose | Method |
| Edification | Topical |
| Thesis Statement |
| Believers overcome the attacks of the enemy by putting on the Armor of God, establishing godly guardrails, fleeing temptation, and faithfully following Christ on the narrow path. |

Firstly, I want to thank Tyler and Sova for this opportunity to speak to all of you on what I believe the LORD has put on my heart.
I am sure we have all seen the image of the angel on one shoulder and the devil on the other. Well, as ridiculous as that image is made out to be, there is some truth to it.
Tyler taught about who the enemy is all the way back in 2022. He explained that Satan is not to be taken as a joke- and that the cartoonish guy with a pitchfork and tail- that's not what the enemy looks like. Additionally, it is dangerous to have this view of the adversary.
In 1 Peter 5:8, it reads as follows-
'Be sober and watchful, because your adversary the devil walks around as a roaring lion, seeking whom he may devour.' 1 Peter 5:8 MEV
The enemy is a lion, always on the prowl and ready to tear apart our flesh. Now, whether you know how easy it is for a lion to tear someone apart, you can be sure that it becomes even easier for them when the flesh of their prey is exposed. A lion has a bite force of nearly 4500 newtons, which translates to a force of up to 1000 pounds per square inch. You may be asking the question right now: how would one protect themselves from that? Especially when it is not just any lion, but instead this is the adversary we are talking about.
Firstly, the most obvious form of protection is armor, which is why the Bible teaches about the Armor of God.

The Armor of God
'Stand therefore, having your waist girded with truth, having put on the breastplate of righteousness, having your feet fitted with the readiness of the gospel of peace, and above all, taking the shield of faith, with which you will be able to extinguish all the fiery arrows of the evil one. Take the helmet of salvation and the sword of the Spirit, which is the word of God. Pray in the Spirit always with all kinds of prayer and supplication. To that end, be alert with all perseverance and supplication for all the saints.' Ephesians 6:14-18 MEV
Belt of Truth
Keeps you from making a fool of yourself; you do not want to be storming into battle holding up your breeches. We often make fools of ourselves when we tell rumors without any acknowledgment of facts, looking just like someone running with their pants below their ankles. It is not a pretty sight.
Breastplate of Righteousness
Demonstrates your values in life- they often had a design that reflected the wearer’s values. I encourage you to ask yourself what your values are. Does your breastplate represent that you are a follower of Christ, striving to be filled with His Spirit in your everyday life?
Boots of Peace
Allows you to know when to run at, run away, or stand firm- and there are times every one of these occurrences. Sometimes it feels like we should run head-on into problems — but that very well may be our pride talking — so pray to be able to discern each situation.
Shield of Faith
Protects you from anything the enemy throws your way. Some of you may recall the illustration at Youth Revival, which demonstrated what happens to the fiery arrows of the enemy when they come in contact with our shields- they bounce right off of us. When we are instructed to be like a goose and let the words of others fall right off of us, or turn the other cheek (both of which are biblical principles), this is what that means.
It is also important to know that a breastplate only protects the front of the wearer, which makes it even more important that we do not go anywhere without our faith. When we are surrounded by evil, our faith will keep us protected from what we cannot see- the enemy’s arrows cannot pierce us in the back if we are carrying even a mustard seed of faith.
Helmet of Salvation
Protects your mind from impurity and points it towards the matters of God.
'The thoughts of the wicked are an abomination to the Lord, but the words of the pure are pleasant words.' Proverbs 15:26 MEV
This likely came from the psalmist David, who, unfortunately, was notorious for allowing wickedness to enter his mind; it was this act that led him to kill a man's wife to take her hand in marriage. It is such an important principle that Jesus Himself further explains it in Matthew 6:22-23
'“The light of the body is the eye. Therefore, if your eye is clear, your whole body will be full of light. But if your eye is unclear, your whole body will be full of darkness. Therefore, if the light that is in you is darkness, how great is that darkness!”' Matthew 6:22-23 MEV
Sword of the Spirit
Apostle Peter was quick with a sword- quick to cut off the ear of Malchus, a servant of the high priest, who went forth to obtain Jesus and led to His crucifixion.
'Then Simon Peter, having a sword, drew it and struck the high priest's servant, and cut off his right ear. The servant's name was Malchus.' John 18:10 MEV
The Sword of the Spirit is no sword at all, but rather it is the WORD OF GOD. We shall go forth and fight with Truth- fight for our families and friends' salvation- fight for what we believe in with Truth rather than violence.
Prayer and Supplication
Prayer: the act of thanking God for His goodness- we have previously learned that to find happiness, one must first learn gratitude. I cannot be happy if I am not appreciative of what has been given to me. We are merely stewards; nothing in our name is truly ours but rather God’s.
Supplication: the act of humbly asking from God- the keyword is humbly- it is not that we deserve such requests but that He loves us and will answer- whether it is the answer we want or not.
There are other coverings we can put on to be protected from the adversary, all of which have been called guardrails. The way we dress is an example of this, ensuring that we are dressing modestly, with gender distinction, and resisting the temptation of vanity (which is an obsession with one's own appearance).
Some guardrails are not as simple, not as black and white; they are unique to individuals, and at IYC, they were referred to as “lines.” The things that affect my walk with God may not affect yours, and vice versa. For this reason, we are to draw lines in our lives and begin to form healthy habits. For example, I myself have an alarm clock in my room- a genuine alarm clock that forces me to get out of bed and turn it off every morning. It is not always convenient, nor is it always appealing, and sometimes I wonder what the point is. To be honest, this is too often the mentality I have during my walk with God.
It should come as no surprise, given that I am both a sprinter in track and a runner of cross country, how much I love to run. People often ask why I enjoy running so much. As a matter of fact, yesterday, after my track meet, I went on a mile run, and when I came inside, sweating, Lily just sort of looked at me in disgust and asked that exact question. Now, while I do enjoy running, there is a little more to the story; I am not only running to get to the finish line, but also to get away from what is behind me. From a literal aspect, the things behind me when I run are people, and I want to get to the end before them because- well- that is how sports work. But from a spiritual sense, I am running from sin and the demons that follow.
When I need to clear my head and readjust with life- typically after I have had a stressful or overall emotional day- I run. I love exercise because it is a healthy form of release; I do not box very often, but when I do, I can feel the anger leaving my body- plus it is way more acceptable than swinging on an actual person.
Now, if you think it is cowardly to run away from the enemy, I do not blame you because I also thought the same thing, but the Bible instructs us to flee. 1 Corinthians 6:18 says to flee fornication or immorality. We see something similar in 2 Timothy 2:22, which instructs us to flee from lust. We often talk about repentance and how it means to turn away from sin, 180º, moving in the opposite direction; in this case, it is much more effective to run than it is to walk, even though it is not always ideal.
I do not mean to get too technical, but it is essential to know what these verses are saying. When we look at the Greek word for lust used in 2 Timothy 2:22, we find epithymia (ep-ee-thoo-mee'-ah), which means a strong desire, craving, or longing for what is forbidden. While oftentimes we associate this word with sexual desire, most individuals will find themselves longing for money, which is also harmful because the Bible says in 1 Timothy 6:10 that the love of money is the root of all evil. 
It is important to note that money is not the root of all evil, but rather, the love for it. Jesus Himself says in Matthew 6:24, “No one can serve two masters. For either he will hate the one and love the other, or else he will hold to the one and despise the other. You cannot serve God and money.”
Walking the strait and narrow, as described in Matthew chapter 7, is not supposed to be comfortable — it is not supposed to be easy — if it were, the whole world would be doing it.
'“Enter at the narrow gate, for wide is the gate and broad is the way that leads to destruction, and there are many who are going through it, because small is the gate and narrow is the way which leads to life, and there are few who find it.”' Matthew 7:13-14 MEV
There is this thought process, or temptation, of well, everyone's doing it, so why can't I? And I am here to warn you that this thought process will get you killed… Maybe you think I am being dramatic or simply giving a metaphor, so allow me to explain further.
When we give in to that mentality and start to believe that being one with the world, even in small portions, is okay, we find ourselves allowing sin into our lives. How many know that the price of sin is death and that for this reason Jesus died on the cross, so we would not have to pay that price ourselves. However, when we sin, we still die until we decide to repent, and it is then that the blood washes over our lives. So yes, I did mean for this to be a metaphor, but it goes beyond that.
I am not sure if you are all aware, but statistics show that suicide rates have constantly increased year after year (except for quarantine). These people all died because they were lacking “something.” Pardon my use of a word so vague, but it is difficult to describe exactly what people are looking for in life. Individuals seek their “next high” and look for their “something” in drugs, which includes, but is not limited to: alcohol, nicotine, marijuana, and the list goes on and on, further than drugs even. Oftentimes, we as humans thrive off others' poor circumstances; we live in a society that I personally believe revolves around the social conflict paradigm: the theory that people are constantly fighting for resources to better themselves.
*Have everyone stand
I have an immense passion for these topics, being a part of Celebrate Recovery here on Thursday nights- addiction is not pretty, but society makes it look so appealing. Commercials for alcohol and nicotine products show the protagonist fitting in with those around them. That being said, that is exactly what happens when we use these products… we fit in. I am sure this sounds ridiculous- you guys are probably like I wondered when he was going to lose it and it only took a few minutes. But it is true, and I will say it again- if you want to fit in with the world, then that can be achieved quite easily- just go join the Babylonian people in their land of confusion. However, they cannot help you find what you are looking for. They can help you find misery and death, but if you want happiness, it is here right now- if you want peace, all you have to do is call on the name of Jesus Christ. While the adversary may be a roaring lion- *point up* we know the real Lion who is also our Lamb. Let's just take some time to pray and ask God to help us find what we are looking for.

Why do we not see very many people with Olympic gold medals? It is because such an award is difficult to obtain and requires an immense amount of dedication.

Before I start, I would like to enter into a time of prayer…	First, I want to say how grateful I am for each of you here today—whether you came to hear what God has done in my life or simply to be in the house of the LORD—thank you for being here tonight.

Later, we moved to Sturgis, Kentucky, and then Sebree, Kentucky, where I had attended kindergarten, first grade, and a quarter of second grade before moving to Cowden, Illinois. This was the first move where I noticed the problems within my parents' marriage.
I soon realized that the reason we had moved so often was because Nathan struggled greatly with his mental health—looking back and based on the opinions of my very own therapist years later, it is clear he struggled with bipolar disorder, and I was just unfortunate enough to see both sides of him at times. It was at Cowden that the man I once called a father threatened to take his life in front of my mom, my older sister, my younger sister, and me at age ten.
This incident was the last straw for my mom. She called Grandma, and with no questions asked, we moved in with her and Papaw. By this time, I was in fifth grade with one semester of the school year left, which I would be spending in Louisville, Illinois. I soon found out that kids can be fairly mean and don’t always appreciate newcomers. I became an outcast, which helped contribute, along with many other things, to the depression I had not realized I had and wouldn’t discover until years later.
Returning to my childhood, it was nice to spend every day with Grandma and Papaw, as well as most of my other family members who lived in the area. My grandma’s house became my escape and my happy place. She was a godly woman who sacrificed her happiness almost daily to ensure her kids and grandbabies were happy themselves.
	Once I finished school, I moved in with my mom and her boyfriend, which is weird to think about because this man would later become my stepdad, and I would refer to him as dad to all of my friends because he always treated me like his own blood. In this new stage of life, I gained a brother and a sister in addition to the two sisters I already had, all of whom have always been good to me. I was able to spend the summer doing all of the things that a dad does with their kids like going to the park and throwing a baseball back-and-forth, all of which I never really got to experience growing up—although admittedly I cannot say Nathan was entirely to blame for that because I do not remember a lot of my childhood.

Despite my twisted and selfish thinking, I’m thankful for this situation because it led to me getting the help I needed.

I know that there can be a bad stigma with doctors and medication—I believe the reason for this is that we as Christians get so caught up in the supernatural that we don’t realize God has already orchestrated solutions in the natural. I can’t say for certain whether or not the medicine helped, but I’m certain that God was working in every moment. There was one night in particular when I was drawing water that I found myself staring into it and contemplating drowning myself. I wasn’t “big” in church by any means, but even then, I knew God was trying to get my attention.
On another occasion, I was up late and alone. I held a pocket knife to my arm and wanted to self-harm, but I couldn’t do it—and I thank God that I couldn’t. I partly thought I was weak in the moment, but again, even then, I knew God’s strength was flowing through me. There were a few other occasions when I tried to fulfill the empty feeling inside with substances such as alcohol and marijuana, but this resulted in me feeling even more numb.

There is a voice in my head speaking lies as empty as the grave; it tries to make me believe that ending it all would be easier than enduring it—that I could solve my problems with one selfish act. I am assured that this is directly from the enemy because it is contrary to God’s Word. Jeremiah 29:11 says, ‘For I know the thoughts that I think toward you, saith the LORD, thoughts of peace, and not of evil, to give you an expected end.’ Christ wants to give us all an expected end, so we can live the life He sacrificed Himself for.

I didn’t know what Apostolics were, but after I found out, I thought you people were a little crazy. However, because of this, I had also never experienced Truth (once again with a capital T) and what God’s Word actually says. I remember growing up and questioning my Sunday school teachers when they tried to teach me about the Trinity and how Jesus, the son of God, was separate entirely from God. No one ever talked about the Holy Spirit as a living entity, but rather as some movement in the atmosphere, as if He were just some weather pattern that could happen at any moment but never did. Later, I learned He is also God.

I’d gone to non-denominational services every other weekend during visitations, but I never allowed myself to grow close to God—after all, I had no clue who He was. I wasn’t born in Truth; I am a first-generation Apostolic, but thankfully, I did not have to figure it all out by myself.

I had this anxious feeling about going, and what I should have done was take it to prayer with God, but I lacked the wisdom and spiritual discipline to do so. That same friend of mine whom I was babysitting with at CR convinced me to go, and I’m thankful she did because it was at camp on June 23rd, 2021, that I received the gift of the Holy Ghost (show photo).

After I was done praying and speaking in other tongues, I had some doubt whether what had happened was of me or God, but there was no other explanation than a supernatural one, so the enemy lost that battle. I didn’t know it at the time, but what ended up being her final words to me were, “Keep up the good work, Jon.” I’m not sure if she realized it, but those words would fuel me in everything I did from that moment on. Nehemiah 6:3 says, 'And I sent messengers unto them, saying, I am doing a great work, so that I cannot come down: why should the work cease, whilst I leave it, and come down to you?' I have decided that no matter what happens in my life, I will choose to continue doing this good work—this great work, even—and that all of the adversities I face are the devil’s way of attempting to stop me, but I won’t let him. I cannot cease to give God all of the glory He is worthy of.

I knew that college was for me because I had always enjoyed school and knew I wanted to pursue either mathematics or computer science. Several of my friends were going to colleges across the state, and some even outside of Illinois. I knew this was something that most people went through, and it was just part of life, but the trouble I was having was that I did not know where I was supposed to go. I have found myself at times doing things for others rather than myself; I am not talking about acts of kindness, but rather how I live my life compared to those around me.

When they informed each student whether or not they had been accepted, I found that I had made it in as an undeclared, which was typical for a freshman. Initially, I was excited, I knew I could dorm with a buddy of mine who also got in, and that with U of I being such an esteemed university for my studies, my opportunities were boundless. It was because of this that I chose to attend Eastern Illinois University, and it has been one of the most mature decisions I have ever made.
Over the past couple of years (or maybe longer), my younger sister has been attending church with me. We worshipped before the preaching, but after the first song, I began to lose that energy. I could not explain why I felt zapped all of a sudden. I didn’t want my emotions to interfere with what God wanted to do, so I did what I could to be open-minded and upbeat during the sermon. In retrospect, I believe that I was under spiritual attack from the adversary because he was scared of what God was going to do in that service and for the rest of my life. I remember a sermon that was spoken during my time in the youth—about how Satan has a target on our backs, but instead of responding with fear, we can respond in triumph because, by the power of God, we have the enemy scared. The decisions I have made are not solely my own but are made only by the direction of God. Had I decided to attend U of I, then I probably would not be in church because I know myself, and I know the lack of self-control I carry. If I weren’t in church, then I could not say whether or not Lily would be either, but I’m thankful I don’t need to worry about that because God is good.

There were many times I needed a ride to church, and thankfully, I had people who were quick to offer. Kristina brought me for the whole year (maybe longer) leading up to receiving my license. There were also many times I needed my way of thinking corrected, and that was indirectly done through a lesson, sermon, or just simple conversations. Being a first-generation Apostolic, and the hurt that I had experienced with my previous church, I often felt, and feel, alone, but I have had a wonderful family here to remind me that we are exactly that: a family.

That is all, thank you.

Praise No Matter the Season
@ Sunday Morning Service [Tagteam] (6/29/25 — United Service)

| Purpose | Method |
| Edification | Topical |
| Thesis Statement |
| Because God is unchanging and worthy of praise in every season, our worship cannot depend on our circumstances or experiences, but must flow from a relationship with Him. |

I would like to give honor to Pastor and Sister Fayth, as well as the entire church leadership. I am very thankful for this church and for the opportunity to come together on this United Sunday and see what God has been doing in every department. Can I simply begin by saying that God is good and worthy to be praised (Psalm 18:3)?
When I was putting together this message, I used this upcoming verse from Psalm 145. After doing so, I came across the exact verse in my devotional, which to me confirms that I am in the Will of God with this message. The verse in reference is Psalm 145:3, which reads:
“Great is the LORD, and [He is] greatly to be praised; And his greatness is unsearchable.” Psalm 145:3 KJV
Moving down to that last verse is Psalm 145, verse 21, it says:
“My mouth shall speak the praise of the LORD: And let all flesh bless his holy name for ever and ever.” Psalm 145:21 KJV
I am confident in saying that God is worthy of all of our praise.
You may be seated this morning.
Praise can be defined as: “the expression of approval or admiration for someone or something.” When we praise God, we are giving Him thanks for all He has done. How many know the LORD has done so much for us?
You know, people can be so quick to praise the things of this world. Whether it be at a sports game or a concert, people are quick to show their adoration for those they admire. However, the level of adoration at baseball games should not exceed that within the life of a believer; the level of adoration at concerts should not exceed that within the life of a Christian. Going further, allow me to make an analogy. There’s nothing wrong with sports so long as they don’t take higher priority than God, and when we have a team that’s doing well, it’s quite normal to be excited and thankful. On the other hand, when that team doesn’t seem to be providing in the ways we think it should, our praise appears to lessen. God should not be treated like how we treat sports teams. God is the same yesterday, today, and forever (Hebrews 13:8), so even when we think He’s not providing in the ways that He should be, He still is and deserves all of our praise. His ways are above our ways and His thoughts above our thoughts (Isaiah 55:8-9). I am reminded of that song that says, “Even when I don't see it, You're workin' \ Even when I don't feel it, You're workin' \ You never stop, You never stop workin'.”
If I can be fully transparent this morning, and I pray that no one takes this the wrong way. I’m not here to brag or boast about how my life is going, but instead, I want to set up the point I’m trying to make. If I could describe my present season of life, I would describe it as a season of mountains. I feel like the LORD is blessing me time after time, and doors are opening. I recently bought a new car, and I’m so thankful that I was able to do so. I’m also thankful that God has sent job opportunities my way, allowing me to pay off said car. So, being honest, praising God is easy for me right now. I have so much to be thankful for, and I am highly aware of that as well.
Something that has entered my prayer life recently is a request to God, asking when that valley comes around, because it inevitably will, that I can maintain the same level of praise I have in these mountains. We cannot only give God praise when it’s convenient for us to do so. He’s still God– He’s the same in every season, even when we aren’t. That’s why it's so important that we focus on a relationship with God rather than an experience with Him.
Don’t get me wrong, I am so thankful for experiences like IYC, Youth Camps, and NAYC. When I return from each one of them, I feel so “spiritually charged.” But, Youth, hear me when I say this: I encourage you to attend all of these when possible. More than attending, I encourage you to look for something you can take home with you. It is so easy to come into the House of the LORD in any setting and experience the presence of God, but then leave without it. The Bible tells us that God wants to dwell within us and fill us with His Spirit, as evidenced by speaking in other tongues. I’m here today to tell someone that when they leave here, they don’t have to rely on any priest to talk to God, because the veil has been torn. When God came down as a man and gave His life for you and me, He set us free! We should talk to God, dedicate time to spend with Him, seven days a week, even eight when possible.
Allow me to close with this: you know why we shouldn’t base our walks with God on experiences? Because eventually, the experiences have to end, but focusing on a relationship and treasuring it over the experience will allow us to give God praise, no matter what season we find ourselves in. I began by saying that God is good and worthy to be praised, and I’ll end by saying God is good and worthy to be praised at all times.

Never Forget
@ Wednesday Night Youth Service (7/16/25)

| Purpose | Method |
| Edification | Topical |
| Thesis Statement |
| Because forgetting God can lead us away from His purpose for our lives, we must never forget who He is, what He has done for us, and the mission He has given us. |

Allow me to begin by honoring Tyler and Sova, as well as Brodey and Alexis, for all they do. Being on the youth committee alongside these guys has shown me how much thought and energy they put into everything. I know they all fill many roles in their everyday lives, but I’m thankful that one of those roles is right here, helping each of us to grow, myself included, by not only teaching the difference between right and wrong, but also making sure the decisions we choose in life are the right ones.
And of course, I cannot forget the one in charge of us all, who keeps us all in line, baby Ellerie.
Lastly, I am thankful that I get to watch you all grow as followers of Christ. I want you all to know that even when you may not see your growth, the rest of us here do.
You all may be seated.
Tonight, I'd like to discuss a few biblical figures with whom you may or may not be familiar. The first two are young men named Hophni and Phineas. These men were the sons of Eli, who was not only a priest, but a judge over Israel. We can assume that with a father like Eli, the two young men grew up having every opportunity to know God. I am sure they attended Sunday school and sat through every church service, watching their father preach. They likely gathered just as we gather here on this Wednesday night, to hear the Word of God. However, the Bible tells us in 1 Samuel 2:12:
'Now the sons of Eli were sons of Belial; they knew not the LORD.' 1 Samuel 2:12 KJV
The term Belial means worthlessness or wickedness, so the verse explains that, despite being present in the presence of the LORD, Hophni and Phineas never made the LORD present in their lives. Let me rephrase: while we can be in God’s presence, we need to go further and let the LORD be within us, filling us with His Spirit.
Returning to the discussion of some more biblical figures. The Bible talks about Samuel, the son of Hannah – she was the mother who prayed so intensely that her voice left her and only her lips moved (1 Samuel 1:13). What she was praying for was a son because she couldn’t have kids, and when the LORD made a way, she dedicated that son unto God. So Samuel was raised by Eli, the priest whom we just discussed, the father of Hophni and Phineas.
Samuel became a great prophet who spent his life following God's Will. It was he who would later anoint Saul to be king, and David thereafter. The Bible tells us in 1 Samuel 8:1-2 that Samuel, like Eli, also had two sons:
'And it came to pass, when Samuel was old, that he made his sons judges over Israel. Now the name of his firstborn was Joel; and the name of his second, Abiah: they were judges in Beer-sheba.' 1 Samuel 8:1-2 KJV
So far, so good, but if we read the next verse, verse 3, it says:
'And his sons walked not in his [Samuel’s] ways, but turned aside after lu·cre [or money], and took bribes, and perverted judgment.' 1 Samuel 8:3 KJV
Once again, we see these young individuals who have so much potential. Holy men and women of God surrounded Joel and Abiah, but it did not matter because they themselves did not have hearts right with God. Allow me to say that it is not enough for those around us to be living for God, because we must have a relationship with Him ourselves.
	My message title tonight is Never Forget. There is a phrase, “Never again, never forget,” which was frequently used by people after the holocaust, which was a time in history, during WWII, when over six million Jews were killed for the sole reason that they were Jewish. This phrase was put into effect to remind everyone of the horror that had taken place, and to ensure something like that would never happen again. People used a phrase similar to “Never forget” after the terrorist attack on the Twin Towers on September 11th, 2001, to honor all of those who lost their lives in said attack.
I mention these war events to say that war is devastating, and that description alone is not enough to paint the complete picture. However, I also want to make the point that there is a war within each one of us, a battle between our flesh and spirit. Scripture goes further to say, in Galatians 5:17,
“For the flesh lusteth against the Spirit [meaning the flesh has a desire that opposes the spirit], and the Spirit against the flesh: and these are contrary to the other: so that ye cannot do the things that ye would.” Galatians 5:17 KJV
The only way we can win this war within us is by remembering who is in control: our higher power, Jesus Christ. In times of war, there is one tactic an enemy favors above all others: cutting off communication between the soldiers and their higher power. How many have gone so long without seeing a friend or family member that when they finally do, they hardly recognize them? That is what Satan wants to do with God and us. The Enemy would love it if you’d stop praying, if you stopped talking with your higher power. Satan wants nothing more than to eliminate conversation because once he has done that, it is so easy for us to forget who Jesus is.
I would like to focus on two additional topics tonight: first, we cannot forget God’s love for us.
God’s Love
In the Old Testament, God’s love is evident in the act of creating humanity. He loved us, and because of that, He formed us from the dust of the earth. In the New Testament, however, God’s love can be seen through His act of coming down as a man, just to be killed by the hand of His creation. Jesus didn’t have to die for us; now hear me, because no one else could have paid that price. So, I am forever grateful that Jesus did. What I am saying is, He could have lived His life as a man, or never come down in the first place, and yet He did, because He loves us.
I have taught on the word 'love' before. My mother raised me with the philosophy that love is the act of choosing to put someone else before yourself. An act that we must make when we first wake up every day. I have recently come to realize that love is more of an action than a feeling, contrary to how our society often portrays it. I have begun to ask myself, Do you love God? And I encourage all of you to ask yourself that question. My response is, of course, I love God, I tell Him so every day. But then I realize that if love is the act of putting another before ourselves, and loving God means choosing His Will over mine, then have I been walking the same walk as my talk? It is easy to say we love God, but we need to ensure that our actions speak the same thing.
If you'll allow me, I feel the need to be transparent tonight, to be vulnerable, and share with you a time when I forgot about Jesus. When I was 12 years old, my parents began going through a divorce. I didn’t understand what was happening; I was too young. But all I started to think was, How could a God in Heaven let this happen? Had I been more spiritually disciplined, I could have taken it to prayer and asked God for guidance, but instead, I took the easy way out. I not only forgot about God, but I denied His existence entirely. I was born in church, I learned about Jesus every week, and I was a preacher’s son, but I turned away from God because it was convenient. I went through some things I should never have gone through, some of which I’m still battling today, because I forgot about Jesus Christ. Heed my warning when I say never forget! Never forget who your savior is, all that He has delivered you from, and where He has brought you out of.
—————————————————————————————————
Before proceeding, I acknowledge that all of my examples thus far have had a religious background; however, I want to emphasize that this precious Truth is for everyone. So what if your father isn’t a preacher? So what if your parents aren’t in church yet? I believe one day they will be! But for the time being, you have a Father in Heaven who is calling you out of the darkness! 1 Peter 2:9-10 reads,
'But ye are a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, an holy nation, a peculiar people; that ye should shew forth the praises of him who hath called you out of darkness into his marvellous light: which in time past were not a people, but are now the people of God: which had not obtained mercy, but now have obtained mercy.' 1 Peter 2:9-10
We should be grateful for the mercy that God has shown us. We should be thankful that God is no respecter of persons and wants to call all of us His children, no matter our past or present situation.
—————————————————————————————————
The second topic we cannot forget is the mission Jesus gave us: to spread the Gospel to others.
The Mission (Sharing the Gospel)
It is crucial not only to our salvation, our chance of being saved, but also to others’ salvation that we do not forget capital-T Truth. We cannot forget the Word because so many people in our communities haven’t heard it! There was a 12-year-old boy out there struggling with anxiety and depression. He was doubting the existence of his Creator when he needed God the most. He contemplated self-harm and whether life was worth living because nothing else made sense to him. Who am I talking about? How do I know all of this? Because that boy in reference is standing right in front of you, and because someone shared the Gospel with me, I can share it with all of you!
I know I am not the only one who has ever struggled. I am sure tonight that some of you have brought something in here, but you have what those in the world don’t - you have someone telling you about how you can be free today. You have someone telling you that there is a name that is higher than any other, a name that is breaking chains and destroying strongholds as we speak. That name is Jesus Christ, and He wants to do the same thing in all of our lives here today.
	Never forget the mission; never forget what Jesus has called us to do: spread the Gospel and share with others how He has set you free, and how they, too, can be freed from the things of this world that bind them.
Even if you forget my name after receiving this message, never forget the Gospel. Never forget that our Creator came down to earth as a man and died a brutal death so that we didn’t have to pay the price for the sin that once held us in bondage. Never forget that Jesus then came back from the dead and conquered that too, so after this life we can have an eternal one with Him. Never forget that God wants to fill us with His Spirit, giving us power and authority over the things of this world.
I believe that Hophni and Phineas, Joel and Abiah, and even my past self, all forgot who the God of their fathers was. In every situation, that mistake was very costly. Do not make that same mistake. These altars are open to anyone who wants to connect with their higher power and remember who He is. I encourage you to ask God to lay someone on your heart who doesn’t know the Gospel. He is calling you to your school to reach your classmates and even teachers. God is calling you to share the Gospel with your coworkers. The LORD is calling you to reach your families and bring them home. All I ask is that we can all answer that call here tonight.

In a world where factories and machines produce so many of the products we see in grocery stores, I’m thankful for a God who does not delegate but instead created us Himself. God did not commission an angel to make us; He could have if He wanted to, but instead He chose to have a greater, more intimate relationship with us. Throughout this morning, I want to discuss that we are the artwork of God. I recently heard a concept proposed by author Jack Phillips, who said something along the lines of this: with us being a masterpiece, breaking down the word “masterpiece, “piece” refers to us as the artwork, while “master” refers to our God, who designed us.
I said I would define the word justification, so here it is: the three-step process which includes {1} repenting of our sins, {2} being baptized by water in the name of our Savior, Jesus Christ, and {3} being filled with His Spirit (evidenced by speaking in other tongues, a language unknown to us, whether that be an earthly or heavenly dialect). When these actions are committed – the first two being our own acts, repentance and water baptism, the third being a total surrender that leads to a heavenly gift from our Father, and all of them being acts of faith – they are all the initial acts of salvation. This instruction is given to us in Acts 2:38 by the Apostle Peter when the people asked what they must do to be saved. It says,
‘Then Peter said unto them, Repent, and be baptized every one of you in the name of Jesus Christ for the remission of sins, and ye shall receive the gift of the Holy Ghost. For the promise is unto you, and to your children, and to all that are afar off, even as many as the Lord our God shall call.’ Acts 2:38-39 KJV
Continuing with the discussion on justification, it occurs from within; change always begins from the inside and progresses outward. For example, let’s say you’re sick; perhaps you have a runny nose, a sore throat, or a cough – those symptoms can be observed outwardly. To treat these conditions, a doctor prescribes medicine, which, when taken, initiates a change within the body before the outward symptoms dissipate.
Looking towards the book of Ezra, we can read about the rebuilding of the temple in Jerusalem. In the book of Nehemiah, which took place some time after the events described in Ezra, we read about the reconstruction of the walls around Jerusalem. Interestingly, the temple was built before the walls were constructed. The Israelites needed to create something to protect before they could create the protections. Similarly, the same must be true for us; before we can put up walls around the temple, we have to let God dwell inside us, filling us with His Spirit, so that there can be a temple to protect.
Paul says it this way in 1 Corinthians 6:19-20,
'What? know ye not that your body is the temple of the Holy Ghost which is in you, which ye have of God, and ye are not your own? For ye are bought with a price: therefore glorify God in your body, and in your spirit, which are God's.' 1 Corinthians 6:19-20
	Our bodies are the temple of the Holy Spirit, of God’s Spirit; we must understand that, by respecting our bodies, we are respecting God’s purpose for them. Earlier, I talked about the importance of self-love. To build on that a little further, Jesus says in Mark 12:29-31 that the greatest commandment of all, equal to the commandment that the LORD our God is one LORD, is to love your neighbor as yourself. How can you love your neighbor if you don’t first love yourself? Jesus was not someone who believed in hate, so He wasn’t saying to hate others as you hate yourself. No, instead, he was assuming that as believers we have learned to love ourselves as God our Father loves us. 1 John 3:14 warns us by saying,
‘We know that we have passed from death unto life, because we love the brethren. He that loveth not his brother abideth in death.’ 1 John 3:14 KJV
Love is an action that leads to everlasting life; the Bible tells us that God is love (1 John 4:8). If we do not possess love, then how can we have God? If we are striving to reach Heaven, then we should strive to have more of our Savior, and that starts with holding more love for ourselves and those around us.
With everything that has been said thus far, one of the main points I want to emphasize this morning is that salvation does not stop at justification; this process is only the beginning of one’s walk with God. Now, I am aware that might sound overwhelming, but I know myself, and any of these youth leaders here today will tell you that their walk with God is their most prized possession. At justification, our lives were changed, but through sanctification, our lives have become even richer. Sanctification is the counterpart to justification; it is the process of purifying or making something holy. In this case, we are making ourselves holy. Romans 12:1-2 tells us this,
'I beseech you therefore, brethren, by the mercies of God, that ye present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God, which is your reasonable service. And be not conformed to this world: but be ye transformed by the renewing of your mind, that ye may prove what is that good, and acceptable, and perfect, will of God.' Romans 12:1-2 KJV
Galatians 2:15-16 says this,
‘We who are Jews by nature [who are holy], and not sinners of the Gentiles [who are fleshly], knowing that a man is not justified by the works of the law, but by the faith of Jesus Christ, even we have believed in Jesus Christ, that we might be justified by the faith of Christ, and not by the works of the law: for by the works of the law shall no flesh be justified.’ Galatians 2:15-16 KJV
	As already stated, we must go beyond our own works, beyond justification, because they do not do enough for us. We must decide to be holy. According to Strong's Concordance, the word for holy in its original Greek writing is hagios (hag'-ee-os). The word for sanctification used in the original Greek is also the same word for holiness, which is hagiasmos (hag-ee-as-mos'). As already stated, it means to purify, to consecrate, and become dedicated to the things of God. We are instructed to present ourselves as holy, as sanctified, every day of our lives. 1 Thessalonians 5:17 says, ‘Pray without ceasing.’ Prayer is the act of connecting with God. In addition to talking with Him, we should also follow the example He set by coming as a man and living a sanctified life.
It has been said that being filled with the Holy Ghost is evidenced by speaking in other tongues, but that is only the initial sign. While still necessary, speaking in other tongues is just part of God's gift; additionally, believers start to bear the Fruit of the Spirit (love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control). In addition to these, the Bible mentions Gifts of the Spirit (wisdom, knowledge, faith, healing, miracles, prophecy, discerning of spirits, diverse kinds of tongues, and interpretation of tongues).
Keep this thought at the forefront of your mind: earlier, we mentioned that justification is the process of restoration; what, then, does that make sanctification?
1 Thessalonians chapter 4 discusses sanctification. It explains that it is the Will of God, for the sake of our holiness, that we abstain from sin and live holy lives. It makes the point to say that the Gentiles don't do this, they don't live a holy life, but no longer are we Gentiles, for all are bought by the blood of the Lamb, and all are called to live a holy lifestyle. The promise in Acts 2:38 is followed by verse 39, which says, ‘For the promise is unto you, and to your children, and to all that are afar off, even as many as the Lord our God shall call.’ If the promise of salvation is unto all of us, so is the requirement of holiness thereafter.
In 1 Thessalonians 4, it is also stated that God has not called us to the unclean, but rather to holiness. One of the laws of the Old Testament, within what is referred to as the Mosaic Law, addresses the dangers of surrounding oneself with the dead and that which is unclean. That law is a type, or shadow, of the things in today's world that are not holy and therefore are unclean; they are not of the Living God and consequently come from the dead of the world. God is not the God of the dead, but the God of the living, so we should follow after the things that give life everlasting.
I have one more passage of Scripture for you all to consider this morning. When I heard Pastor quote this, it brought the entire lesson together for me. In Jude 1:1-2, it reads,
‘Jude, the servant of Jesus Christ, and brother of James, to them that are sanctified by God the Father, and preserved in Jesus Christ, and called: mercy unto you, and peace, and love, be multiplied.’ Jude 1:1-2 KJV
	I mentioned earlier how I heard a message about being restored to the image of God through justification. In that moment, I concluded that sanctification must then be the preservation of said image. I had no recollection of this verse in the Bible. I would have read it several times in my Bible reading, but it wasn’t until now that it stuck out to me. This verse tells us that sanctification is preservation, because it says that we are sanctified by God and preserved by Jesus. Years of studying Scripture have taught me that Jesus is God, which means, by that logic, preservation is sanctification.

We were all made in God's image. While justification is the process of restoration, sanctification is the process of preservation. We must cherish God's artwork, learning to love ourselves and those around us. We must understand that we cannot stop at restoring ourselves to His image; afterwards, we must preserve His image by pursuing a life of holiness. Let’s pray and ask God to help us commit to the acts of justification if we haven’t already, and to guide us in pursuing a lifestyle of holiness.

The Uniting of Brothers
@ Tuesday CMI (9/30/25)

| Purpose | Method |
| Edification | Topical |
| Thesis Statement |
| Because God created us uniquely yet calls us into unity, we must set aside differences and demonstrate Christ’s love by intentionally connecting with and caring for others. |


Today, I would like to begin by discussing two brothers in the Bible, their names being Esau and Jacob.
We get our first understanding of these two men in Genesis chapter 27. Issac, their father, asks his eldest son, Esau, if he would prepare a last meal for him as he was about to die of old age. As a result, Issac promised to give Esau his blessing, as was custom in their culture for a father to do unto his eldest son. While Esau is out hunting to prepare venison for his father, the younger brother, Jacob, swoops in to steal his brother’s blessing. The Bible says that when Issac grew old, his eyes began to dim, and he had trouble seeing. This was to Jacob’s advantage because he and his brother were vastly different from one another, despite being twins.
The name Esau, in its original Hebrew context of the Old Testament, means “hairy.” Esau was so hairy that Jacob, having smooth skin, had to use the skins of goats on his hands and neck when he posed as his brother. In the end, Jacob steals Esau’s blessing.
Now, I have never had my blessing stolen, but I can imagine what it feels like. You know that feeling when the last yogurt cup is in the fridge and your sister eats it. You told her not to, you said, “Hey Lily, I call dibs on the last yogurt.” She says, “Yeah, no problem, Jon, I don’t really like that kind of yogurt all that much anyway.” Then BOOM, it’s just gone. Everybody’s been there… In the same manner, Esau would have been beyond angry with Jacob. Knowing this, Jacob flees from his homeland to avoid his brother’s wrath.
In Genesis chapter 33, several chapters later, a lot of time has passed. The brothers come face-to-face after all this time, and they are arguably even more different now than in chapter 27. Jacob has been renamed Israel and has begun fathering the first 11 of his 12 children, each of whom would later have a tribe named after them. Jacob’s new name, Israel, means “God prevails.” Just like his father and grandfather, Jacob is doing his best to follow God; no longer is he referred to as Jacob, which means “deceiver.” In a similar sense, Esau has also been renamed; he is known as Edom. The difference is that Esau is the first ancestor of the Edomites, a nation that worshipped several gods, none of which were Israel’s Yahweh.
With that being said, if we read their encounter in Genesis 33:1-4, it is unexpected to say the least. It says,
'And Jacob lifted up his eyes and looked, and behold, Esau was coming, and four hundred men with him. So he divided the children among Leah and Rachel and the two female servants. And he put the servants with their children in front, then Leah with her children, and Rachel and Joseph last of all. He himself went on before them, bowing himself to the ground seven times, until he came near to his brother. But Esau ran to meet him and embraced him and fell on his neck and kissed him, and they wept.' Genesis 33:1-4 ESV
Instead of hatred, the brothers showed love for one another, even after all those years, despite their differences in physical characteristics and religious beliefs. In the end, they embraced, showing love and unity.
All of us here tonight come from diverse cultures; we have all had different upbringings. Many of us come from various churches, and some of us may not have a church to call home. Not one of us is the same, and yet we all sit here in this room because we want connection and, whether we realize it or not, connection is necessary. We are all different because God made us unique from one another. I have heard the analogy that, as a body of believers, we cannot all function as the eye because then we would have no hearing. We cannot all be an ear because then how are we to smell? These two brothers put their differences aside, showing love and unity just as all of us should, not only with one another in this room but with everybody on this campus.
Scripture has this to say about love:
'The second [greatest commandment, equal to the first] is this: ‘You shall love your neighbor as yourself.’ There is no other commandment greater than these.' Mark 12:31 ESV
'Anyone who does not love does not know God, because God is love.' 1 John 4:8 ESV
	What would happen if we all actively demonstrated love to everybody in our vicinity? This is not to say that we are demonstrating hate. Still, if we were constantly aware of the impact we could have by carrying a warm smile or showing a kind gesture to someone, I really think we could make a significant difference, even if it’s only seen within that one person’s life.
Scripture has this to say about unity:
'Knowing their thoughts, he said to them, “Every kingdom divided against itself is laid waste, and no city or house divided against itself will stand.' Matthew 12:25 ESV
'complete my joy by being of the same mind, having the same love, being in full accord and of one mind.' Philippians 2:2 ESV
	Going beyond a simple act of kindness, we can all build a relationship with the people around us and learn more about who they are. Ask yourself this: when was the last time I connected with a total stranger? Just as Esau and Jacob were brothers, we are all sisters and brothers in Christ; we have the same heavenly Father and are made of the same elements, the same struggles, we share the same emotions – let’s use this to our advantage and connect with someone new, showing them the love that the Bible instructs us to show.

There are Methods to a Miracle

| Purpose | Method |

I want to start by saying thank you to Tyler and Sova, and Brodey and Alexis, for their leadership and passion for the things of God. I am also thankful for the Student Leadership Team for helping us make the Friday night youth rally special. By a show of hands, how many had a great time?

When putting this lesson together, it took a turn. I wanted to talk about burdens, but when I finished pouring the overflow into this message, the LORD had instead burdened me to study out miracles. Today, I want to discuss how “There are Methods to a Miracle.”
'On a certain day, as He was teaching, Pharisees and teachers of the law were sitting nearby, who had come from every town of Galilee and Judea and from Jerusalem. And the power of the Lord was present to heal the sick. And behold, some men brought in a bed a man who was paralyzed. They searched for ways to bring him in and lay him before Him. When they could not find a way to bring him in, because of the crowd, they went up on the roof and let him down through the tiles with his bed into their midst before Jesus. Have them sit if they are not already.
So here we have these men carrying their paralyzed friend. There was no way for them to reach Jesus because the house's entryway was so crowded. I love their innovation – their quick thinking – they decided to lower their friend down through the roof. We have to stop and think, though, not only did these men have to climb onto the roof, but they had to carry their friend and his bed up there with them. Then, they had to tear into some other man’s house, taking apart the straw roof so they could create an opening large enough to lower the man in his bed down. I think it’s easy to gloss over that part of this story; roofs are meant to be closed (can I get an amen?). If the roof weren’t closed, it would not have kept out the rain, or more likely, the critters in that land. So these men tear into this person’s house, who has kindly agreed to host this Bible study. Jesus never said Kingdom work was easy, but if I were the owner of the house, I wouldn’t have expected this. If you would, allow me to continue reading, verse 21 says:
'The scribes and the Pharisees began to question [and here we see them questioning the act of Jesus], “Who is He who speaks blasphemies? Which is easier, to say, ‘Your sins are forgiven you,’ or to say, ‘Rise up and walk’? But that you may know that the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins,” He said to the paralyzed man, “I say to you, rise, take up your bed, and go to your house.” Immediately he rose before them, and took up that on which he lay, and departed to his own house, glorifying God. ' Luke 5:21-25 MEV
This is where the miracle takes place in the story. The verses we read earlier were the setup for the miracle, but the setup is almost just as important, if not more important, than the miracle itself. I read a book called “God Has a Waiting Room” by Kim Haney, and she talks about how, as soon as a storm hits, we start praying for it to go away. I have heard Pastor preach a message about praying at all times — the Bible says to pray without ceasing (1 Thessalonians 5:17) — we cannot simply pray during a storm, a trial, or tribulation. Still, we should be praying before and after the storm, preparing for the next one. Kim Haney says in her book that we take the storm for granted. God often communicates with us in storms; it is in those situations that we better learn who He is and what character He has. The disciples did not realize the full extent of who Jesus was until a storm hit them; only then did they understand He was more than just some man, for He held power over even the wind and the waves of the earth (Matthew 8:27).
Continuing our discussion on the setup for a miracle, Bro. Shaun Strader, one night at CR, explained to me that there are methods to a miracle. If we stop and take a look at the miracles God performed in the Old Testament and in His life on earth in the New Testament, the miracles themselves are unbelievable, but their methods are even crazier.
In the Old Testament, we read about God parting the waters in three instances.
Parting of the Red Sea
'Then Moses stretched out his hand over the sea, and the Lord caused the sea to go back by a strong east wind all that night, and made the sea dry land, so that the waters were divided. The Israelites went into the midst of the sea on the dry ground, and the waters were a wall unto them on their right hand, and on their left.' Exodus 14:21-22 MEV
Moses was the vessel God used in this instance; he took his hand and stretched it out over the Red Sea, and the waters parted, allowing the Israelites to flee from the Egyptians, all while walking on dry ground.
Parting of the Jordan River
'14When the people set out from their tents to cross over the Jordan, the priests were carrying the ark of the covenant before the people. When the carriers of the ark came to the Jordan, the feet of the priests carrying the ark dipped into the edge of the water. (Now the Jordan overflows its banks all the days of the harvest.) Then the water that flows down from upstream stood still and rose up in a heap very far away at Adam, the city beside Zarethan. The water that flows down toward the Sea of Arabah (the Dead Sea) stopped and was cut off. The people crossed over opposite Jericho. 17The priests carrying the ark of the covenant of the Lord stood firmly on dry ground in the middle of the Jordan, and all Israel crossed over on dry ground until the entire people completed crossing over the Jordan.' Joshua 3:14-17 MEV
	As soon as the priests carrying the Ark of the Covenant entered the Jordan River, it split in two, allowing them to walk across on dry ground to the other side. Moses had to stretch out his hand for the waters to part, but here we see the water split, seemingly on their own. I love verse 17 when it says the Ark of the Covenant stood firmly on dry ground, and I love that you and I here this morning can stand firmly on the Word of God!
'8Then Elijah took his robe and rolled it up and struck the water, and it was divided from one side to the other. Then the two of them crossed on dry ground. 14And he [this is Elisha now] took the robe of Elijah that fell from him, and struck the water, and said, “Where is the Lord, God of Elijah?” When he had struck the water, it parted from one side to the other, and Elisha crossed over.' 2 Kings 2:8,14 MEV
	When parting the Jordan River, Elijah took his robe and struck the river, and it parted for him and Elisha. Then, in the same instance, Elisha strikes the Jordan a second time with the robe of Elijah, calling out unto God, and it parts again. In the previous two cases, the Bible doesn’t say anyone called out to God before the waters parted; this is unique to this story.
	Allow me to move into the miracles of Jesus in the New Testament. 
Healing of the Blind Men
'He came to Bethsaida. And they brought a blind man to Him and entreated Him to touch him. He took the blind man by the hand and led him out of the town. When He had spit on his eyes and put His hands on him, He asked him, “Do you see anything?” He looked up and said, “I see men as trees, walking.” Then again He put His hands on his eyes and made him look up. And he was restored and saw everyone clearly.' Mark 8:22-25 MEV
	In Mark 8, Jesus spits in His hands and wipes them on the blind man’s eyes. Now, I love my Savior, but I am not too sure how I’d feel about His spit on my face. When the blind man still couldn’t see clearly, Jesus did it again, restoring the man's sight on the second go.

Jesus then instructed the man to wash in a nearby pool, and it wasn’t until then that the blind’s sight was restored. 
Going back to the story of the paralyzed man, the reason the setup was so important — the reason the method for the miracle must occur before the miracle — is that the method demonstrates faith. God doesn’t send us through storms to force us to turn toward Him; He might do it to remind us that He’s there even in the storm, but He’s not a dictator; He doesn’t want us to suffer. Storms come our way, assuming we didn’t invite them in, so that our faith can be strengthened. There is one last verse in the story of Luke chapter 5 that we must look at, allow me to read verse 26:
'They were all amazed, and they glorified God and were filled with fear, saying, “We have seen wonderful things today.”' Luke 5:26
We won’t always understand the methods for a miracle, but I believe that there’s always a reason for each one. In this story, it was because of the method that people believed in God, glorifying Him and fearing Him, meaning they kept His commandments thereafter.
I want this lesson not only to have intrigued you all as we learned what the Bible says, but also to prepare you for when God asks you to do things that don’t seem “normal” to society. Nothing godly will appear normal to the world because the world views everything in a worldly sense. In Acts 17, the people of the world had said the Apostles and their doctrine had turned the world upside down. Tyler has taught before that it is a matter of perspective because what is upside down to the world is right side up in the eyes of God.
As we close, can we pray and ask God to help us continue to study His Word? Ask Him to prepare us for His Will and use us, no matter how weird the circumstances may seem from an outside point of view. Let’s do that now…

Grateful Despite the Storms
@ Tuesday CMI (11/18/25)

| Purpose | Method |
| Edification | Topical |
| Thesis Statement |
| Because God’s love and faithfulness remain constant in every season, believers should develop a lifestyle of thanksgiving that is not dependent on their circumstances. |

Let’s go ahead and start with a time of prayer.
Hey everybody! I hope all of your classes are going well. It’s the home stretch. We get a break from being in-person for all of next week, then we come back and finish strong in the last two weeks before finals. Does anyone else feel like this semester has just flown by? Thanksgiving is next week, and honestly, I’m here for it.
I want to talk about thanksgiving, not so much about the holiday, but more about the act of giving thanks. But first off, let me lay a foundation.
The first Thanksgiving took place in 1621 in Plymouth, Massachusetts. It was a harvest feast, and the crops had just been placed on the dinner table after vigorous amounts of work with sowing and reaping. It was a time to be thankful; the land was fulfilling the needs of its people, and who isn’t grateful when food is involved?
The concept of gratitude did not start at Plymouth Rock; it dates back much earlier, to the times of Biblical writings. Specifically, in Leviticus chapter 7, we can read about a thanksgiving sacrifice; there were rules and regulations for each offering unto God, and an offering of thanks was no different.
Animal sacrifice is under the Old Covenant; it’s something that we, as New Testament believers, as Christians in the modern day, no longer practice. However, understand that our Savior, Jesus Christ, is the same “yesterday, today, and forever,” meaning that if God so highly valued giving thanks in the Old Testament, He still cares that we give thanks today.
1 Thessalonians 5:16-18 instructs us by saying:
“Rejoice always, pray without ceasing, give thanks in all circumstances; for this is the will of God in Christ Jesus for you.” 1 Thessalonians 5:16-18 ESV
The commandment here, “give thanks in all circumstances,” that’s something I want to spend some time on. It’s really easy to be thankful when life is going smoothly. We are all optimists when the outcome looks to be in our favor, but as soon as our life takes an unexpected turn, we are quick to lose our spirit of gratitude. It’s not always easy to give thanks in all circumstances; that’s not what I’m saying. I have had my fair share of family loss, and it was not easy to still thank God for what I did have and, more importantly, who He was. There were times, if I’m being honest, that I didn’t thank God at all, even though I had so much to be thankful for. Reading about Job's story, we see him give thanks to God even at the lowest point of his life. Job not only lost all of his family, but he also lost every possession he had. Nonetheless, he thanked God because deep down he knew the LORD had not forsaken him, even when he couldn’t feel God.
“Continue steadfastly in prayer, being watchful in it with thanksgiving.” Colossians 4:2 ESV
Again, we see this teaching that we are supposed to pray without ceasing; we are to be steadfast in prayer. This verse also adds that we should be watchful with thanksgiving. It is so easy to point out the issues in life, to point out where things didn’t turn out in our favor, but what we need in this world are people who look toward the good in life when so many only see the bad. We need people who say, “This didn’t go in my favor, but God will work it for my good.” Or they say, “I don’t understand why any of this is happening, but at the end of the day, I still have a God I can turn to who loves me, who provides, and who died so I could be free from my sins.”

No, instead, the love I want to discuss is the love of a Father and the love among His children. Before we can go any further, we must take a look at the definition of love. How does the Bible define love? Let’s find out.
1 Corinthians, chapter 13, is often referred to as the “love chapter.” It follows after chapter 12, which discusses the Gifts of the Spirit. Because each of the gifts requires love before they can be appropriately used, Paul dedicates chapter 13 to explain what love is, to ensure no one abuses their God-given abilities. I’ll be summarizing verses 4–7, but I encourage everyone to find the time and read these passages of scripture, from which I am pulling.
1 Corinthians 13:4-7:
Patient
Kind

Always protects
Always trusts
Always hopes

Allow me to be the first one to say that none of these things I just listed are easy. If loving people were easy, this world would be a much better place, but we have to be the change we want to see, which means putting in more effort than those around us.
In the Gospels, Jesus was asked what the greatest commandment of all was. Matthew 22:37-38,
'Jesus said to him, “ ‘You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart, and with all your soul, and with all your mind.’ This is the first and great commandment.' Matthew 22:37-38 MEV
	One thing I love about this scripture is that Jesus is quoting the Old Testament. This isn’t the first time that God gave this commandment to His people. Moses wrote these exact words in Deuteronomy 6:4-6.
'Hear, O Israel: The Lord is our God, the Lord alone! And you shall love the Lord your God with all your heart and with all your soul and with all your might. These words, which I am commanding you today, shall be in your heart.' Deuter­­onomy 6:4-6 MEV
	With authority, Jesus declares this next part,
'And the second [commandment] is like it: ‘You shall love your neighbor as yourself.’ On these two commandments hang all the Law and the Prophets.”' Matthew 22:39-40
	That is a lot to comprehend: all that Jesus has just laid on these people hearing His message. If I could speculate for just a moment, I think that life got a little too comfortable for these followers of God over the years. Perhaps they loved God and taught their families to love God because they loved their families, but outside of that, they missed this important truth, so Jesus revealed it to them. This walk with God is a narrow road, traversed by few, but notice how scripture still makes note that it is traversed by few rather than none? Nobody’s alone in their walk, and it’s our job to make sure each person understands that they aren’t alone in life.
	The New Testament was originally written in Greek. There are two commonly used words for love in the Greek language: agapaō (ag-ap-ah'-o) and phileō (fil-eh'-o). Going into this study, I knew that agapaō was a love we were supposed to have towards God. I also knew that phileō was a kind of love we held for one another (because Philadelphia is the “city of brotherly love”). So I had this assumption that when Jesus gave these commandments, He was saying to have an agapaō kind of love for Him and a phileō type of love for our neighbor, but that wasn’t the case. Jesus used agapaō in both instances, so I further studied its meaning. Agapaō means to love in a social or moral sense; it’s the highest degree of love that a person can have. Phileō seemed to me like a sort of casual love, like, “Ah man, I love that guy, but sometimes…” fill in the blank. So not only are we to hold the highest degree of love toward God, but we are to hold it toward our brothers and sisters as well.
Earlier, I said we would learn about the love of a Father and the love among His children. How many know that they are a child of God? No matter what background you come from, you are His. No matter what you have done in life, you are God’s, and He loves you with that agapaō kind of love. I mentioned the “chapter of love,” also known as 1 Corinthians 13. Some translations use the word "charity" for "love," but in Greek it is translated as agapaō, which we have been talking about.
I mentioned that love keeps no record of wrongs. God loves us so much that when we commit wrongdoing and come to Him in repentance, and find ourselves baptized in Jesus’ name, He washes our sins away. God isn’t a god that waits for us to mess up so He can strike us down; He’s waiting to catch us when we stumble from sin. His love is patient, His love is kind– His love is the protection we need from the principalities of this world.
Love is action; it’s not a feeling, though the world may define it that way. The Bible quotes Jesus by saying in John 14:15,
'“If you love Me, keep My commandments. ' John 14:15 MEV
Note the word agapaō used here once again. We don’t just love God by saying, our actions speak louder than our words. We love God by obeying His commandments. What are His commandments? Well, the Bible is full of them, but the greatest of all is this: love the LORD with everything you have in you, and love your neighbor as yourself.
	This is a three-part commandment: we are to love God, we are to love our neighbor, but there is also the assumption that we have self-love. We are told to love our neighbors as we love ourselves. How can one love somebody if they first do not have the care and appreciation for themselves? If I can be honest here this morning, there was a time when I didn’t love myself, I didn’t like who I was, or the things I’d done. I wasn’t happy with the person staring back at me every morning in the mirror. But when I met God, all of that changed. I realized that I was fearfully and wonderfully made (Psalm 139:14), that the God of the universe took His time to form me from the clay of the earth, and has had plans for me long before my parents were even alive. God knows the plans He has for each of you. You are loved by God even when you may not love yourself.
	God’s love for us is not self-seeking; God loves us even though there is the possibility that we do not return said love. Love isn’t asking, “What do I get in return?” Love is simply caring for this person, and even though they might not reciprocate that love, knowing that God loves them, and we do too.
	1 John 4:19-21 says,
'We love Him because He first loved us. If anyone says, “I love God,” and hates his brother, he is a liar. For whoever does not love his brother whom he has seen, how can he love God whom he has not seen? We have this commandment from Him: Whoever loves God must also love his brother.' 1 John 4:19-21 MEV
	The word agapaō is used again to indicate the degree of love being described here. The Bible questions the logic of not loving our brothers and sisters in Christ. We see one another every day, and if we can’t carry a love for one another, how can we carry a love for God, whom we do not see? There is a relationship between faith and love. Allow me to use this quote:
“Faith rests on the past, hope looks toward the future, but love works in the present.”
We have faith in God because of who He was and what He’s done for us. We have hope because of who we know He will be and all that He will do. But we have love because of who He is and what He is doing right now. So ask yourself this morning, how can I love God more? How can I love myself and my neighbor more?

When it comes to the names and titles that God uses throughout Scripture to identify Himself, we see a plethora of these. Listening to an old sermon on YouTube, I heard preacher Johnny James cite that there are 1,024 names and titles referencing God. This likely not only includes the compound names which I intend to get into in a minute, but it also refers to the metaphors that refer to God like how He is our Shepherd and our Strong Tower.

And when it comes to titles, I am a son, but I’m not the son. I hope to be a father one day, but I’m not the Father. That’s what’s unique about God; for anyone else, their title describes their position and their name identifies them as a person. For God, He was often identified by His titles. I believe that this is why the following encounter happened in Exodus 3:13-14:
'And Moses said unto God, Behold, when I come unto the children of Israel, and shall say unto them, The God of your fathers hath sent me unto you; and they shall say to me, What is his name? what shall I say unto them? God simplified things for Moses by educating him that He was all things that ever were, all things that are, and all things that ever will be. Taking a forward look at the New Testament, God makes things even simpler for us by educating us on the power and importance of His name, Jesus.

We don't care how it looks / Jireh will provide it

'and I appeared unto Abraham, unto Isaac, and unto Jacob, by the name of God Almighty, but by my name JEHOVAH was I not known to them.' Exodus 6:3

God is Good

| Purpose | Method |

| Thesis Statement |
| God is inherently good; therefore, no matter what trial, exile, or season of spiritual silence we face, we can trust that He is actively working behind the scenes for our good. |

I’m thankful for the opportunity I have to stand before you all and deliver the Word of God. I’m thankful for Brodey and Alexis and their dedication to the things of God. I know they invest a lot of time and energy into everything they do, and that much of it goes unseen, so if we could just let them know we’re thankful for them and their leadership.
I want to open with one verse of Scripture, it comes from Romans 8:28 which says,
[Rom 8:28 KJV] 28 And we know that all things work together for good to them that love God, to them who are the called according to [his] purpose.
	My title for tonight is simply this, “God is Good.”
Let’s begin with a time of prayer…
From the beginning of creation, God has been making all things good. From Genesis chapter 1:

| [Gen 1:4, 10, 12, 18, 21, 25, 31 KJV] 4 And God saw the light, that [it was] good: and God divided the light from the darkness. ... 10 And God called the dry [land] Earth; and the gathering together of the waters called he Seas: and God saw that [it was] good. ... 12 And the earth brought forth grass, [and] herb yielding seed after his kind, and the tree yielding fruit, whose seed [was] in itself, after his kind: and God saw that [it was] good. ... 18 {regarding the two great lights} And to rule over the day and over the night, and to divide the light from the darkness: and God saw that [it was] good. ... 21 And God created great whales, and every living creature that moveth, which the waters brought forth abundantly, after their kind, and every winged fowl after his kind: and God saw that [it was] good. ... 25 And God made the beast of the earth after his kind, and cattle after their kind, and every thing that creepeth upon the earth after his kind: and God saw that [it was] good. ... 31 And God saw every thing that he had made, and, behold, [it was] very good. And the evening and the morning were the sixth day. |
We see here that whatever the LORD creates, is good. When God steps in, you know that good is going to come out of the situation. I don’t think the problem we have comes from not believing that God is good– because all of us have at least some understanding that God is so good to us. The real problem is that we don’t always feel God, and in those moments we can forget about His goodness.
I want to take a little bit of time examining this verse of Scripture, Jeremiah 25:9 says,
[Jer 25:9 KJV] 9 Behold, I will send and take all the families of the north, saith the LORD, and Nebuchadrezzar the king of Babylon, my servant, and will bring them against this land, and against the inhabitants thereof, and against all these nations round about, and will utterly destroy them, and make them an astonishment, and an hissing, and perpetual desolations.
Notice here that the LORD speaking through His prophet Jeremiah, calls Nebuchadrezzar, the King of Babylon, His servant. This is a very powerful statement when we begin to understand what is unfolding around this time. When we read the accounts from the book of Daniel– whether it be Daniel in the lions’ den or the three Hebrew boys and the fiery furnace– those accounts all take place during Judah’s Babylonian exile. God’s people were taken from their land and forced to live their lives alongside the Babylonians for seventy years.
So it’s interesting that the LORD uses Jeremiah to explain that Nebuchadrezzar is a servant to God. Now, the Bible wasn’t saying that Nebuchadrezzar was a servant of God– the Babylonian king didn’t follow or even know the God of Judah. So what we read about here is God demonstrating His power and control over even the worst of circumstances.
An exile meant that God’s children would have to leave the land that they loved. They would become surrounded by a culture that was unfamiliar to them. Their government and their kings no longer would hold any power. Meanwhile, false prophets were rising up and giving false hope that the exile would be short-lived.
I don’t know that these circumstances were as bad as what the Israelites experienced in Egypt, but this exile would have been the current generation’s equivalent to the Egyptian bondage.
Now, I can make some speculations as to why the LORD allowed this exile to happen, because remeber, His Word calls Nebuchadrezzar His servant. My first speculation is that without the Babylonian exile, my favorite book, the Book of Daniel, wouldn’t exist— at least not in the way that it does today. Secondly, the God of Judah would not have been revealed to the Babylonian people like we read about. But like I said, I can only speculate, and because we’re not God, we don’t really need to know why He does what He does, we are just called to trust Him.
Job had a very similar experience to the children of Israel, one that required a lot of trust. In Job 23:8-9, Job says,
[Job 23:8-9 KJV] 8 Behold, I go forward, but he [is] not [there]; and backward, but I cannot perceive him: 9 On the left hand, where he doth work, but I cannot behold [him]: he hideth himself on the right hand, that I cannot see [him]:
How many of us have been there? I know I have. I’ve had my moments in life where I have asked, “God, why did this happen? God, how could you let this happen. God, what are Your plans?”
Now, I won’t stand up here and say that we must never have these thoughts of doubt, because inevitably we all will. However, I will say that we should not dwell too long on these thoughts. It’s one thing for these thoughts to enter our mind, but we shouldn’t entertain them.
Imagine what would have happened if the Israelites further pursued their feelings of doubt when their promised land was so close? What would have happened if Abraham and Sarah stayed within their familiarity and comfortableness of doubt when their promised son was to be born in their old age.
I’d also like to point out what Job says in that passage we read, notice his attitude, because that’s what makes all the difference. He said, “On the left hand, he doth work, but I cannot behold [him].” Job recognized that even though he could not see God, He was still working. Let me say that even though you may be in a situation where God appears to be absent, He is always working.
It’s important that we understand the thoughts we have towards God. Do we truly believe that He would abandon us? The Bible tells us what He thinks of us. In Jeremiah 29:11 it says,
[Jer 29:11 KJV] 11 For I know the thoughts that I think toward you, saith the LORD, thoughts of peace, and not of evil, to give you an expected end.
	This famous piece of Scripture that we hear about so often becomes even greater when we understand the context to which it was written.

God reminded them that although this was going to be hard, He would still be by their side and His divine Will would still conquer all. There was a worry that they would not flourish in this new lifestyle, that they would stop having kids, and that God’s people would begin to grow weak.

I have come to tell you all that during those moments, continue to pray, continue to read your Bible, and continue to live your lives– never let the world break you down and try to tell you that God has forsaken you— that life is pointless— because God has never left you, and He never will.

Have you ever experienced the same feeling that Job had, that no matter where you looked, you couldn’t feel God? Have you ever had a season in your life that made you feel like you’d been exiled?
In those experiences, how did God reveal Himself? What made you realize, or what happened that revealed to you, that God has never forsaken you?

	I hope you all spent some time reflecting over the goodness of God. I want to say that if you couldn’t think of anything to talk about during the small group questions, you’ll have plenty to testify of as you continue your walk with God.
	I also want to point out that if the LORD did it for those in your small group, He can do it for you. Also, if God did it for you, if He made a way– brought provision or healing– then He can do it again. God is not a one-hit wonder. If you look across many of the miracles throughout the Bible, many of them took place more than once.
The water was split at the Red Sea for Moses but the Jordan also split for Joshua.
In Matthew 15, Jesus multiplied the fishes and loaves for 5000 men plus women and children. He did the same thing again in the next chapter for 4000 men plus women and children.
Throughout the four Gospels, there are at least five separate encounters where Jesus healed the blind.
In Luke 7, the widow’s son was resurrected and in Luke 8 Jairus’ daughter too was brought back to life.
And this isn’t even to mention the resurrection of Lazarus or Jesus Christ Himself after His death on the Cross.
God did it for them, and He can do it for you. When we say God is a waymaker, a provider, or a healer– we can say it with the utmost certainty because not only did He do all of those things time and time again for these people we read about, but He’s working all things for good in our lives still today.
So before we wrap up here tonight, let us pray and give God whatever it is that we are struggling with. Let us trust Him in whatever season we may find ourselves in…

God of the Hills and Valleys
@ Wednesday Night Youth Service (9/16/26)

| Purpose | Method |
| Evangelical | Topical |
| Thesis Statement |
| God’s power is not determined by a geographical location or topographical features, He is the same whether we are in seasons of hills or valleys. Both seasons are crucial to the lives of Christians, and by understanding each of them, we can be better as a result. |

I’m thankful for the opportunity that I have to speak with you all this evening about what the LORD has laid on my heart. I’m also thankful for each and everyone of you, that you chose to spend your Wednesday night in the House of the LORD, I can promise you that there is no better way to spend it.
Tonight, I want to read from 1 Kings 20, verses 23 and 28. As you’re turning there, allow me to say that I’m not so far removed from the season of life that each of you find yourself in today. I’m still a student and am aware that along with that comes homework, projects, and tests to study for. I know that each of you have friends who want to hangout, whether that be in-person or on a video game. But nevertheless you all chose to be in the LORD’s presence and spend time with the body of Christ, and for that I really am proud of all of you and your commitment to the things of God.
The Word of the LORD says,
[1Ki 20:23, 28 KJV] 23 And the servants of the king of Syria said unto him, Their gods [are] gods of the hills; therefore they were stronger than we; but let us fight against them in the plain, and surely we shall be stronger than they. ... 28 And there came a man of God, and spake unto the king of Israel, and said, Thus saith the LORD, Because the Syrians have said, The LORD [is] God of the hills, but he [is] not God of the valleys, therefore will I deliver all this great multitude into thine hand, and ye shall know that I [am] the LORD.
	Tonight, I am going to speak about the “God of the Hills and Valleys.”
Let us pray…
	Our opening text takes place during King Ahab’s rule over Israel. The two kingdoms of Israel and Judah were split apart, with Judah’s kingdom still seating the heirs of King David on the throne. Within this chapter, we find that Israel and Syria are going to war. As a result, the Syrians show us multiple misunderstandings that they have in regards to the God of Israel.
First and foremost, the Syrians mistakenly pluralize God. These people say that, “Israel serves the gods of the hills.”
Now, I know that we in the Pentecostal-Apostolic movement stress on the Oneness of God quite frequently. With that being said, I wouldn’t blame you if you felt it was done so to the point of ad nauseam– if you maybe felt that we talk about Oneness “too much.”
Maybe none of us think that, but to prevent that thought from ever arising, allow me to use a quote I once heard which said, “We shouldn’t scream what the Bible whispers and whisper what the Bible screams.” Now, what that means is that we should understand the degree to which the Bible talks about certain subjects and ask ourselves if we are matching that degree. One of those things that Scripture repeats over and over again is the Oneness of God.
Something that other denominations don’t scream enough is,
“Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God is one LORD:” (Deuteronomy 6:4), or
“For in [Christ] dwelleth all the fulness of the Godhead bodily” (Colossians 2:9), or that
“...no man cometh unto the Father, but by [Christ Jesus]” (John 14:6).
They’ll scream baptism in the name of the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit but fail to tie it back to the rest of Scripture in that Jesus is the name that Matthew 28:19 is referring to.
The God of Israel is so unique because of His Oneness aspect. While many nations worshipped a pantheon– a collective of gods– Israel had only one God, and were to never divide their worship or service unto the other gods.
The reason that we emphasize the Oneness of God is because it is such a unique aspect when compared to false gods of the ancient world. It’s also an aspect that is debated today. People across Christian denominations will debate whether God is one, two, or three persons; they then will debate what the definition of a person is.
So my hope is that all of us, when we hear a message talking about Oneness, we find ourselves rejoicing that we have the Truth that God is one. I hope that we never grow tired of the messages referencing Oneness, but instead celebrate that we have the answer that is so highly debated among Christian scholars.
The second mistake that the Syrians make, and the one that I want to spend the rest of tonight talking about, is in regards to the sovereignty of God. The Syrians had lost the first battle against the Israelites while on top of a hill. With their limited understanding, the Syrians had assumed that this meant the LORD was a god of the hills and mountains, and that by changing geographic locations into the flatlands, that they would claim victory.
You see, Syria was one of those nations I mentioned earlier that served a plethora of gods. They had dozens of them that mythologists would consider headliners like we do with the Greco-Roman Olympian gods. But the Syrians likely had hundreds of gods that they worshipped over the years of the Bible being written. So it is no surprise when they assume that Israel’s God is one over the hills and mountains. And to be honest, if I was losing a battle such as this one, I too would make up some excuse as to why.
To further His sovereignty, the LORD sends a prophet unto King Ahab explaining that the battle will be won by Israel because of the mistake by the Syrians. The prophet states that the LORD is both God over the hills and the valleys, and was about to show that to the Syrians.
The LORD delivers the Syrians into the hand of Israel, proving that His power is not determined by a geographical location or topographical feature– He is the God of the hills and valleys. I want to break these two subjects up a little bit and correct some misunderstandings we sometimes make when it comes to God.
Hills
We find ourselves in a season of hills when life is going well. We have a good understanding that God is in control during this time. Maybe life is going just as we planned it, or maybe it’s a goodness beyond our imaginable. Whatever the case, I would caution each of us, if we find ourselves here for the long-term. Part of the reason we should pray, no matter the season, is to determine whether or not we’re supposed to be in it.
Don’t get me wrong, God wants what is best for us. However, what is best for us isn’t often what we want. Looking throughout Scripture, what was best for the Apostles wouldn’t have been anyone’s first choice (likely not even second or third choice). They were persecuted, Stephen was martyred, James and Peter were imprisoned, James died as a result of that, and Paul had a huge list of things that he had experienced.
Now, I’m not saying I want any of this to happen to anybody, nor am I saying that it has to, but I am saying that life shouldn’t be comfortable. There is a reason it is called the season of hills. We are not meant to stay in it, sooner or later we should begin to notice the hill began to slope downward. After a few more steps, we will naturally find ourselves in a valley.
Valleys
Don’t mistake these valley seasons as life going bad. It may not be going our way, but God is still in control. The exile of Babylon is an extreme example of a valley season for the Israelites. For us, times of loss are an example of a valley season. These experiences in our lives are a part of life. They’re not comfortable, and they’re not enjoyable, but by endeavoring until the end, we’re closer with God as a result.
I mean think about it, if it weren’t for the valley, how would we ever know how great the hilltops were? If it wasn’t for nightfall, how would we know the excitement of a new day? If it wasn’t for the storm, would we ever be reminded of the promises of God after seeing a rainbow up in the sky?
The three Hebrew boys had faith already, but their valley season through the fiery furnace gave faith to Nebuchadnezzar. Daniel knew God deserved his prayers, so when he was sent into the lion's den for them, King Darius had begun to call on a God he only knew through association with Daniel.
Maybe your valley season isn’t for you. Yes you have to go through it, and we all will have to go through a valley at some point in our lives, but not only do we have a chance to come out stronger, so do the people who see us going through it.
Stand with me here tonight…
I don’t know what season of life you’re in tonight. Maybe you’re doing great. You’re in a season of hills. But remember to pray. Ask God for guidance for when you find yourself in a valley.
Maybe you’re in a valley here tonight, you should already be praying but remember Scripture when it says, “Weeping may endure for a night, but joy cometh in the morning.” There’s never been a day in my life that the sun didn’t rise, it may have been cloudy, but eventually those too had to part way for the divine hand of God to reach into my circumstances, pick me up, and place me on higher ground. 
Let us find a place to pray– I may not know what season of life you’re in, but God does. God not only sees your present season but He knows the reason you’re going through it, He knows the plans He has for you, and “they are of peace, not of evil, to give you an expected end.” Let us go before the LORD in prayer…

Throughout these next two Sundays, as we talk about the Beatitudes, you are going to hear me reference LJ Harry. He’s a great man of God and he writes a lot of books, one of which is titled Blessed Are: A Practical Look at the Beatitudes. I used his book to study out the Beatitudes and put these lessons together.
So first off, let’s answer the question, what are the Beatitudes?
The Beatitudes come from the Sermon on the Mount, Jesus’ first message of His earthly ministry. Each of the following Beatitudes start with, “Blessed are…” To be blessed is to be happy; to be blessed is to be content. The kind of happiness and contentment that we’ll be talking about aren’t anything like the world could ever offer; what we’re talking about is joy. The Beatitudes aren’t telling us how we can feel an emotion, they’re helping us to obtain a spiritual attitude— to make the conscious decision to be happy and content no matter where we might be or what we might have. When we’re filled with the Spiritual Fruit of Joy, which can only come from the Spirit of God, nothing can take that away from us.
I have heard it said that the Beatitudes are like the rings of a ladder, before we can reach the final ring of “Blessed are they which are persecuted for righteousness' sake…” We must take a look at the other seven. So let’s get started with the first beatitude in Matthew 5:3…
The Poor in Spirit
'Blessed are the poor in spirit: for their's is the kingdom of heaven.' Matthew 5:3
It can be easy to misquote this verse. I've heard it said, “Blessed are the poor.” If your finances are anything like mine when I was your age, you’d like for this verse to say “blessed are the poor.” However, as we will come to find out, there is a much greater find in this text. No matter how much money you have, whether you are poor or rich, you can be blessed– you can be joyful, even still.
Including the second half of the verse, it reads, “Blessed are the poor in spirit…” Now, the Bible refers to two different types of poor– first there is the working poor and then there is the begging poor. 
The working poor are the people who work everyday and they just get by. I would imagine Peter to be a great example of the working poor; Him and his brother Andrew were fishermen, they worked everyday for a living, they got by, but were never rich.
The begging poor, however, had to rely on other people’s generosity in order to survive. We see a lot of these people are lame or have been dealt what we could consider a “bad hand” in life. Maybe they were widowed women like Naomi and Ruth, having no husbands and no sons that they rely on the generosity of Boaz in order to eat.
The kind of poor that this beatitude is referring to is the begging poor. We must come to understand that without the grace of God, we could not make it in this life. It was because of God’s grace that He chose to form us out of the dust of the earth, despite the disobedience He knew would take place in the Garden of Eden. It was because of God’s grace that He moved upon the Virgin Mary, lived thirty plus years, was tempted just as we are tempted, and died on the Cross by the hand of those whom He created. It was because of His grace that Jesus became our sins despite never committing one Himself. Jesus took on the lies and yet had never been a liar. Jesus took on the manipulating while having never been a manipulator. He did all of that out of grace because as the preacher of the Beatitudes, He understood more than anyone that without His grace, we would not make it.
When we carry this attitude that we are poor in spirit, that we are beggars of the Spirit, we begin to understand that we can never get anywhere without God. We no longer go to church or district events because we are excited about who will be speaking or singing, but instead we gather to hear the anointing found in the Word and time of worship. We don’t go for entertainment, but to be fed the spiritual manna that only God could provide.
Taking a look at the next beatitude found in Matthew 5:4…
They That Mourn
'Blessed are they that mourn: for they shall be comforted.' Matthew 5:4

She was barren, she could not have any kids and all the while her husband’s other wife– it was a different time, it was a different culture– Peninnah mocked Hannah and provoked her. So Hannah sought after the LORD and prayed so valiantly, she was so determined, that the priest thought she was drunk. The priest, a man of God, could not even recognize the woman was praying– that’s how much emotion and spirituality was involved in her praying.
It isn’t just women who cried in the Bible. Boys, the world is going to tell you that men don’t cry. It’s going to tell you that you’re more of a man for holding back tears and less of a man if you let them through, but that’s just not biblical.
Abraham wept when he buried his wife, Sarah, in Genesis 23:2.
David and Jonathan wept in 1 Samuel 20:41 when Saul sought to kill David.
In Acts 20:37, all the people wept, including Paul, when it was time for the Apostle to depart from Ephesus.
The shortest verse of the Bible, John 11:35 says, “Jesus wept,” which was His response to the death of his best friend Lazarus, who Jesus loved greatly.
If God-incarnate understands the importance and the necessity of weeping in this life, then we should too.
Because God came to Earth as a man, we have the promise found in the second-half of this beatitude, we have the comforter. The mediator that Job wished he could have, to be the inbetween for himself and God– it’s the Holy Spirit.
John 14: 16-18 talks about the  Holy Spirit being a comforter. Jesus explains that the Holy Spirit will be a forever comfort. Jesus is prepping His people for the day when He departs from them. Jesus understands that He cannot always be with them as God-incarnate so He informs everybody that He will return in the form of His Spirit, which can be in all places all at once, including in each and every one of us if we would allow Him to fill us with His Spirit.
The type of mourning that we are talking about is related to the godly-sorrow spoken about in 2 Corinthians 7:10,
[2Co 7:10 NKJV] 10 For godly sorrow produces repentance [leading] to salvation, not to be regretted; but the sorrow of the world produces death.
	This godly sorrow is what we call conviction, it’s counterfeit– what we sometimes misidentify as conviction or conviction as it– is condemnation. Condemnation comes from the world, it will break us down and separate us from God, but conviction is from God, it will build us up and draw us closer with Him. The difference between the two can be seen in the lives of Jesus’ disciples. Judas had betrayed Jesus by showing the Roman soldiers where He prayed. Later, Judas hangs himself because of the regret he feels. Simon Peter betrays Jesus by denying ever knowing him on three different accounts. Instead of letting his regret eat him away as it would Judas, he runs to Jesus.
Repentance is turning away from our wrongdoing, from the things of this world. Condemnation will cause you to run further into the world, but conviction will turn you around, running into the direction of Jesus. Conviction isn’t comfortable, it’s like Jesus in the Garden of Gethsemane– it’s that oil press. Conviction is like Job’s experience of being refined but coming out as fine gold. After finding repentance we have reached the first step in salvation followed by baptism in Jesus name and the infilling of His Spirit– which is the Comforter that we talked about earlier.
LJ Harry writes in his book, that “the first beatitude breaks the bad news to us: that we are poor in spirit; that our spiritual balance is overdrawn. The second is our right response to our spiritual balance statement. To realize we are nothing without God prompts us to weep.”
Now that we have conquered the first two rungs of this eight-runged ladder, let us walk up to the third beatitude found in Matthew 5:5…
The Meek
'Blessed are the meek: for they shall inherit the earth.' Matthew 5:5
Imagine everybody who was hearing this message preached by Jesus. He has told them that happy are those who are spiritually broke, emotionally broken, and now He is saying to be happy they must all be gentle.
To understand the situation a little more, let’s take a look at the four main groups who heard this message. You can think of each one of these groups like you would groups at school– there are the athletes, the band kids, the nerds, or the ranchhands. In our small, tight community, you might know somebody who’s all four of those things. But the point is, in Jesus’ time, there were the Pharisees, the Sadducees, the Zealots, and the Essenes.
Pharisees
The Teachers of the Law
Middle-class
Disliked the Gentiles, viewing them as beneath God’s holy people.
Disliked publicans, or tax collectors, seeing them as disloyal to their people.
As an accountant, the Pharisees would have despised me.
Neutral toward Rome, believing and trusting God to set things right in due time.
Believed in both the written and oral Torah (or the Pentateuch, the first five books of the Old Testament).
Sadducees
The Wealthy Aristocrats
Upper-class
Loved Rome because it gave them political power and wealth.
Believed in the written Torah, but not what was traditionally spoken from generation to generation.

Zealots
The Violent Rebels
Hated Rome, they believed paying taxes to a pagan king was a sin against God, the one, true King. They were willing to kill and destroy if it meant ending Roman rule.
Essenes
The Separatists
Ignored Rome, focusing entirely on themselves and the holy text.
In John MacArthur’s book, Twelve Ordinary Men, he states that the Pharisees wanted a miraculous Messiah; the Sadducees wanted a materialistic Messiah; the Zealots wanted a military Messiah; the Essenes wanted a monastic messiah. Meanwhile, we see Jesus approaching as a meek Messiah.
	Meekness is not a weakness. The word in the Greek for meek is Praus. It is a term that described soothing medicine which doctors gave to fevered patients, the gentle wind experienced by sailors, and even a colt after it has been broken in (or tamed). The truth is, meekness is all of these things. Meekness is not weakness, but power under control.
	All types of medicine have a prescribed dosage because taking any more than prescribed will have unintended effects. A cool breeze is nice until it becomes a full fledged tornado. A colt, when unbroken, will do much more damage than one that has been tamed.
	Meekness is a teaching that Jesus’ brother, James, knew very well; he writes in James 3,
[Jas 3:5, 7-8 KJV] 5 Even so the tongue is a little member, and boasteth great things. Behold, how great a matter a little fire kindleth! ... 7 For every kind of beasts, and of birds, and of serpents, and of things in the sea, is tamed, and hath been tamed of mankind: 8 But the tongue can no man tame; [it is] an unruly evil, full of deadly poison.
James says that the tongue is like a fire, it starts out small but if not put in check, it will have firetruck after firetruck trying to douse every area of our life with water.
This is why meekness is so important, it’s power under control– our tongue holds a lot of power and needs to be broken just like a baby horse. However, notice that James tells us, unlike all the animals of the earth, our tongues cannot be tamed by man. Thankfully, another passage of Scripture teaches us about the Fruit of the Spirit, which contains something called self-control.
Pursuing a relationship with God, coming face to face with the process of salvation– repentance followed by baptism by water and baptism by God’s Spirit– will result in self-control along with many other God-given characteristics.
But meekness isn’t just about suppressing power, it’s power under control, it’s the redirection of power for the Kingdom of God. Regarding the tongue, instead of breaking others down, build them up by saying, “Brother, I love those shoes!” or, “Sister, let me just say that your purse really compliments your outfit.”
Meekness is a superpower– we can all be superheroes– but we must always remember that it is God who grants us the power to be meek.
Let’s move on to our last Beatitude for today…
They Which Do Hunger and Thirst After Righteousness
'Blessed are they which do hunger and thirst after righteousness: for they shall be filled.' Matthew 5:6
	Hungry people are desperate people. It’s crazy what people will do for food, especially when they’re starving. I won’t get into details on cannibalism but if you look throughout history, it has existed.
	There are so many emotions that rise up when we’re hungry, has anyone in here every been hangry (I know Lily has)? There is just something about hunger– but once we’re filled, we’re completely different people, new creatures I might add (2 Corinthians 5:17).
	There is one man in the Bible who was very hungry. However, his hunger was different from that which we have been talking about; He was hungry for God. He was so hungry that he put his reputation and career at risk to talk to Jesus. That man’s name was Nicodemus.
	One night, Nicodemus secretly meets up with Jesus. He says, “Rabbi,” or master, "we know that thou art a teacher come from God: for no man can do these miracles that thou doest, except God be with him.” Nicodemus is quite flattering, but Jesus is quick to respond with,
[Jhn 3:3 KJV] 3 Jesus answered and said unto him, Verily, verily, I say unto thee, Except a man be born again, he cannot see the kingdom of God.
	Nicodemus is confused, “Born again? Say whattt?” So Jesus instructs Him a little more.
[Jhn 3:5 KJV] 5 Jesus answered, Verily, verily, I say unto thee, Except a man be born of water and [of] the Spirit, he cannot enter into the kingdom of God.
This promise from Jesus comes to pass on the day of Pentecost, where Peter is asked, “How must we be saved?” And he replies,
[Act 2:38 KJV] 38 Then Peter said unto them, Repent, and be baptized every one of you in the name of Jesus Christ for the remission of sins, and ye shall receive the gift of the Holy Ghost.
When we are hungry for God, He will fill us with His Spirit. When we have met up with our convictions, and are led to repentance, He will then fill us with His Spirit. I know many of you are probably hungry right now as we speak, but let me tell you about a dish that never gets old, that you never grow tired from eating.
I have this struggle, maybe you guys do too, where I get hungry day after day. I’ll have had three meals one day and then the next day I could eat three more. Do you guys ever feel that way? Yeah, I’m sure all of you do because that’s completely normal.
	When we’re full we don’t stay that way very long.
	The same is true of our spiritual lives. 
	Jesus tells the woman of Samaria in John 4 when she denies Him a drink from the well, “If thou knewest the gift of God, and who it is that saith to thee, Give me to drink; thou wouldest have asked of him, and he would have given thee living water. Whosoever drinketh of this water shall thirst again:”
The thing about salvation, about being filled with God’s Spirit, that’s only the beginning. We are called to pursue righteousness for the rest of our lives. So when you hunger and thirst after the things of God, you’re blessed, because as a result of that, you will be filled. You’ll always grow hungry and thirsty– it’s in our nature to depend upon our Creator for survival– but it doesn’t take long for God to fill you up during a time of prayer, Bible reading, worship, teaching, or preaching. Continue seeking that which is righteous, feeding your soul on heavenly things– that which will make the difference in eternity.
The world has prepared plenty of dishes as well, they try to compete for the dish of the LORD but they are only temporary. When we feed ourselves full of the junk food that the world offers us– whether it be death scrolling short clips, looking at posts from our favorite influencers, trying to excel at that latest videogame, etc… it is only a matter of time before we become hungry again, perhaps even more hungry than we were before. Seek after righteousness, satisfy your soul with something that will last.
Happy are the poor. Happy are the sad. Happy are the humble. And happy are the hungry. We’ll pick up next week with the next four beatitudes. Until then, I encourage you all to read these verses from today, Matthew 5:3-6, and think about how you can put each one into practice. I love you all. The Merciful
'Blessed are the merciful: for they shall obtain mercy.' Matthew 5:7
In Jesus’ day, some called mercy a, “disease of the soul.”
Forgiveness was not something that was normal in the culture at that time, nor is it widely popular in today’s world.
On the topic of forgiveness, Peter asks Jesus this question in Matthew 18, he says,
[Mat 18:21 KJV] 21 Then came Peter to him, and said, Lord, how oft shall my brother sin against me, and I forgive him? till seven times?
	Peter was being quite generous here. He knew that Rabbis taught their followers to forgive three times before considering themself off the hook (Amos 1:3-13). However, Peter also knew Christ was more generous than your average Rabbi, so he jumped up to the number seven. To Peter, it probably looked like a noble amount of forgiveness– it was the perfect number afterall, modeling the days of creation. But Jesus takes it a step further when He says,
[Mat 18:22 KJV] 22 Jesus saith unto him, I say not unto thee, Until seven times: but, Until seventy times seven.
	Jesus wasn’t literally saying, you better start counting, how close until they hit that quota? No, instead He was making the point that it doesn’t matter how many times we’ve been wronged, we are still called to forgive. I do want to caution you all, however, if someone has wronged you 490 times, maybe stop spending time with them. Forgive them, yes, indulge them, no.
After Jesus teaches Peter about forgiveness, He tells a story to help everyone better understand the subject.
There was this servant who owed the king ten thousand talents. We don’t know how much money that equates to in the modern dollar, but we know it was so much money that the servant could not pay it back within his lifetime. The king threatens the servant’s family, taking his wife and children into custody until the debt could be repaid.
In that moment, the unforgiving servant did all that he could do; he came to the end of himself, bowed before the king and asked for mercy. The king was moved by the servant’s actions. He tells the servant that his debt was forgiven– completely erased. The servant leaves the king’s palace with such relief, as if the world had just been lifted from his shoulders.
God is similar to the king in the sense that when we confess our sins, He will forgive us of them. 1 John 1:9 says,
[1Jo 1:9 KJV] 9 If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us [our] sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.
God forgives sins, but we must first repent of the wrongs we have committed. God is our righteous judge, it would not be right for Him to pardon us unless we decided to change. Can you imagine if a judge let a criminal back onto the street despite them having no remorse for their actions? That’s not very judge-like.
We have to have a moment like the servant before the king, realizing that we have messed up and that the only thing that can save us is the mercy of God.
In his book, “Blessed Are…” LJ Harry references a song from an unknown artist which reads,
He paid a debt He did not owe	I owed a debt I could not pay	I needed someone to wash my sins away;	And now I sing a brand new song,	Amazing grace,	Christ Jesus paid a debt that I could never pay.
We could never pay the price of sin, we needed a spotless lamb, for the price of sin is death. Given that none of us are spotless– or without sin– Jesus paid that price for us. He didn’t have to, but He did because of His love for us. We are called to show that some love to others, which is something the servant in the earlier story failed to do.
After the servant was forgiven by the king, he decided to follow up on a  person who owed him money. He came to the man who owed him one-hundred denarii, and once again we aren’t sure how much money that was, but we believe it could have been paid much more easily than the ten thousand talents. It was likely that this man could pay his one hundred denarii if given one hundred days to do so. The servant, however, wanted his money and he wanted it now. This man got down on his knees and begged for mercy, not so that he would not have to pay, but he simply wanted an extension. The servant was not feeling very merciful despite just receiving mercy from the king, so he put the man into the debt prison.
When the king heard about this, he was furious, he called for his guards to bring the unforgiving servant into his palace where he condemned the servant’s behavior.
[Mat 18:32-34 KJV] 32 Then his lord, after that he had called him, said unto him, O thou wicked servant, I forgave thee all that debt, because thou desiredst me: 33 Shouldest not thou also have had compassion on thy fellowservant, even as I had pity on thee? 34 And his lord was wroth, and delivered him to the tormentors, till he should pay all that was due unto him.
	Jesus then tells us that the Kingdom of Heaven is the same way, if we become hypocrites after being forgiven by God for not forgiving our fellow man, we shall be punished.
Mercy gotten begets mercy given, or at least it should. We must forgive others as we have been forgiven by God.
Happy are those who have had to forgive. Notice the people who are happy are those who have been hurt rather than those who have never experienced that kind of pain?
Forgiveness is special because it heals the person forgiving before it ever heals the person receiving forgiveness.
So far, we have begun to realize we have nothing without God, which causes us to mourn, to be meek, and to be hungry for Him and what He has to offer. We have also just learned that nobody owes us anything, that we are only here by the grace of God.
Let us move up this ladder unto the next rung of our Beatitudes…
The Pure in Heart
'Blessed are the pure in heart: for they shall see God.' Matthew 5:8
Notice that this beatitude isn’t, “Blessed are the pure on the outside,” but instead talks about the heart.
Purity and holiness should not be burdens, but rather are protections for us.
Jesus is trying to teach these people, and us as readers, that holiness must start on the inside before it can ever be viewed on the outside.
Out of all the beatitudes, this one must have really gotten the Pharisees attention. They acted as if purity started on the outside. They dressed real nice and always wore smiles across their face– but none of it was genuine, it was all for show.
I have heard it said that holiness is like a set of guardrails on either side of the road that you see when you’re driving. You don’t want to get too close, otherwise you’ll get dinged up and might even drive off this strait and narrow path. You could also describe holiness as being like an electric fence, if you get too close to that line and rub against the fence you’ll grow numb to the feeling and forget the fence is even there. Whether you see holiness as a guardrail or an electric fence, forsaking it may not kill you, but it also won’t lead you to eternal life.
Purity is an undivided devotion to God. This means we must get our priorities straight, focus on God. James 1:8 warns us not to split up our focus or be double minded because,
[Jas 1:8 KJV] 8 A double minded man [is] unstable in all his ways.
Pure in-hearted people don’t have to worry about tripping over their disguised personas. They aren’t worried about what others may find after taking a close look at their life because they have nothing to hide.
We aren’t pure in heart to please our parents, or to please Brodey, Alexis, or even Pastor Geuin– we are pure in heart to please God. Jesus loved us so much that He died on the cross for us, if holiness pleases Him (and it does), then I want to be holy.
The Word of God makes us pure:
[Jhn 15:3 KJV] 3 Now ye are clean through the word which I have spoken unto you.
[Eph 5:26-27 KJV] {Christ died for us} 26 That he might sanctify and cleanse {us} with the washing of water by the word, 27 That he might present it to himself a glorious church, not having spot, or wrinkle, or any such thing; but that it should be holy and without blemish.
We are called to be in the world and not of it (John 17:14-18), which calls for a certain balance. James 4:4 says,
[Jas 4:4 KJV] 4 Ye adulterers and adulteresses, know ye not that the friendship of the world is enmity with God? whosoever therefore will be a friend of the world is the enemy of God.
We must find balance between living in this world while still representing the God of the Heavens. This isn’t our permanent home, but we still have to take care of it and love the people within it. We can start loving people by telling them about our Creator who first loved them (1 John 4:19), how He died to save them (John 3:16, 1 Timothy 3:16), and how He is calling for us to live in relationship with Him, separating ourselves from sin (Galatians 5:24, 1 Corinthians 15:31).
David was a man after God’s own heart, so we should really pay attention to what he writes in Psalm 57:7,
[Psa 57:7 KJV] 7 My heart is fixed, O God, my heart is fixed: I will sing and give praise.
We should also take note of his writing in Psalm 139:23-24,
[Psa 139:23-24 KJV] 23 Search me, O God, and know my heart: try me, and know my thoughts: 24 And see if [there be any] wicked way in me, and lead me in the way everlasting.
	When we have become pure in heart, our hearts have been fixed, they have returned back to how they were supposed to be before sin entered the world. Because sin has entered the world, we must find humility in asking God to search us and remove what was not placed there by Him.
	Once we do that, we can find ourselves starting to become pure in heart, to which the blessing is given, “that they shall see God.” 1 John 3:2 reads,
[1Jo 3:2 KJV] 2 Beloved, now are we the sons of God, and it doth not yet appear what we shall be: but we know that, when he shall appear, we shall be like him; for we shall see him as he is.
	When we grow close to God, we return back to the image that He created us in. We aren’t perfect, and we won’t find ourselves being exact replicas of our Creator in this life, but one day we shall see God for who He is and be like Him.

Some parts of greater visibility, others have greater utility, but all serve a purpose.”
The human body can survive without a foot but not without a beating heart. However, one should not have to choose between the two; a healthy body would have both. “Likewise, the church could never have existed without the ministry of apostles, prophets, and teachers– yet it could limp along for a while without helps, administrations, and public messages in tongues.”
A healthy, fully functional church will desire and acquire all of God's gifts for His body.
Session 3: Love in the Body of Christ;
Chapter Six: 1 Corinthians 13 – Love in the Exercise of Spiritual Gifts
The Greek word for love in this chapter is agape (ἀγάπη), which, in its highest sense, means divine love, selfless love, sacrificial love, and love without expectation of return. In some Bible translations, agape was translated as charity, which has since lost its true meaning.
The only acceptable motive for operating spiritual gifts is love, but what is love?
Patient
Kind
Not envious
Not boastful
Not proud
Not rude
Not self-seeking
Not easily angered
Keeps no record of wrongs
Does not delight in evil
Rejoices in the truth
Always protects

Always perseveres
By claiming that supernatural gifts have already ceased (e.g., tongues),  then the other gifts would have to cease as well, such as prophecy and partial knowledge. This erroneous thought would imply that we have perfect sight and knowledge and, by implication, no longer need faith or hope.
When the LORD returns for us, only then will we no longer need faith or hope, for our journey will be over.
“Faith rests on the past, hope looks toward the future, but love works in the present.”
Session 4: Gifts of Revelation;
Chapter Seven: Wisdom, Knowledge, and Discerning of Spirits
The Greek word for wisdom is sophia (σοφία). “Wisdom” means “understanding of what is true, right, or lasting; insight; … common sense; good judgment.”
Knowledge is an understanding of facts, but wisdom is an understanding how to use facts to make good decisions.
Word of Wisdom: the supernatural gift of a portion of divine insight, judgment, or guidance for a particular need.
Paul was not a sailor, but divine wisdom told him in Acts 27 that it was dangerous to sail any further. Using human judgement, the Roman centurion continued on anyway, right into a tempest.
The Greek word for knowledge is gnosis (γνῶσις). “Knowledge” means “familiarity, awareness, or understanding gained through experience or study; … the sum range of what has been perceived, discovered, or learned.”
Word of Knowledge: the supernatural gift of a portion of divine information for a particular need.
“Discernment” means "keenness of insight and judgment.”
There are three possible sources of a spiritual activity: God and His angels, the devil and his demons, or the human spirit.
Discerning of Spirits: the supernatural gift of perceiving the spiritual motivations for an action, or what type of spirit is at work.
In Acts chapter 16, there was a girl whom Paul had discerned was demon-possessed, and she kept praising Paul and the men with him. Paul had known to cast out the demon so as not to relate the ministry of God with the ministry of devils.
The gifts of Revelation are potentially available to every Spirit-filled believer.
We don't necessarily have to define every gift that is given by God, so long as we recognize that it is of God’s Spirit.
Session 5: Gifts of Power;
Chapter Eight: Faith and Miracles
Faith: confidence, trust, acceptance without tangible proof, reliance, commitment.
“Every child of God possesses saving faith and lives daily by faith. Additionally, every Christian should manifest faith or faithfulness as the fruit of the Spirit. I Corinthians 12 describes a supernatural gift of faith that Trent sends the faith required for salvation and Christian living.”
Gift of Faith: The supernatural ability to trust God, or to inspire trust in God, for a particular need or circumstance.
Miracle: An event that appears inexplicable by the laws of nature and so is held to be of supernatural origin or an act of God.”
To God, all things are possible, and what is supernatural to us is merely standard procedure for him.
The word “working”  [energema (ἐνέργημα)] indicates a specific operation of God.
Working of Miracles: The supernatural intervention of God that transcends the laws of nature in a situation and operates through or with a human vessel.
We cannot give credit to luck or coincidence; they are not our God.
“If anyone will not work, neither shall he eat” (II Thessalonians 3:10).
Session 5: Gifts of Power;
Chapter Nine: Healing
“To heal means to restore to health or soundness; cure; … to set right; … repair; … to restore (a person) to … wholeness.”
Healing can refer to physical, mental, and spiritual restoration.
The healing in 1 Corinthians 12 refers to physical and mental conditions beyond the spiritual and emotional healing that comes with being a new creature in Christ.
Gifts of Healing: Various forms of supernatural cure or restoration from illnesses, diseases, injuries, and other impairments.
Note that this is the only gift in the plural because there are multiple conditions that can be healed, and many methods for healing.
What Jesus did for the early church, He will do for the church today.
Sometimes healing comes with time, but God still orchestrates it. Our bodies have naturally occurring healing properties, but were created by God.
Someone may receive partial healing and need continued faith and patience for full healing.
Sometimes God miraculously delivers us from a trial, but other times he allows us to go through it.
The doctrine of positive confession: a teaching stating that if you believe for healing, then it will happen.
We should not judge others who are sick, but we should examine ourselves to see if God is trying to chasten or teach us through an illness.
Sometimes God may use an illness to accomplish a specific purpose in our lives or the lives of others.
Session 5: Gifts of Power;
Chapter Ten: Faith for Healing
The word “sick” comes from the Greek word astheneo (ἀσθενέω).
Anointing with oil reminds everyone that healing comes by the power of the Holy Spirit.
Session 5: Gifts of Power;
Chapter Eleven: The Laying on of Hands
From these New Testament examples, we can identify several important purposes of the laying on of hands:
Blessings
Healings
Reception of the Holy Spirit
Ordination to and Anointing for Service
The laying on of hands focuses people's faith receive a promise from God at a particular time.
Session 6: Gifts of Utterance;
Chapter Twelve: Tongues and Interpretation
The Greek word for “tongues” in 1 Corinthians 12-14 is glossa (γλῶσσα), which refers either to the organ or to a language.
The gift of tongues is the gift of a supernatural utterance and one or more languages unknown to the speaker. We see it used in three ways:
 as the initial sign of the baptism of the Holy Spirit
 in personal devotions
 in public utterance to be interpreted
The gift of tongues is different from the initial speaking of tongues given in the baptism of the Holy Spirit.
We cannot judge our salvation or spirituality by how often we speak in tongues.
The Greek word translated "interpretation" is hermeneia (ἑρμηνεία), from which we get the English word hermeneutics, meaning principles of interpretation. To interpret means "to explain the meaning of " or "to translate orally.”
God gives interpretations according to the speaker's mental ability, understanding, and expectation.
Different people receive interpretations in different ways.
Session 6: Gifts of Utterance;
Chapter Thirteen: Prophecy
The Greek verb propheteuo (προφητεύω) means “ to speak under inspiration.” It also picked up the meaning of “to foretell events.”
Prophecy is the gift of a supernatural utterance directly from God in the language of the speaker and hearers.
Only the recipients of a prophecy can decide what it means for them.
Unlike the Bible, prophecies are not infallible.
Session 6: Gifts of Utterance;
Chapter Fourteen: I Corinthians 14 – The Vocal Gifts in Public Worship
The solution to abuse is not disuse but proper use.
Someone who speaks in tongues speaks to God, while someone who prophesies speaks to others.
Tongues are very valuable for personal devotion.
Prophecy primarily benefits believers.
While God is infallible, no human being is.
The gifts are subject to proper use or misuse, and it is our responsibility to use them properly.
 The Greek word translated "decently” is euschemonos, which comes from euschemon, meaning “decorous, proper, noble, honorable.”
We should earn the desire for all the gifts of the spirit, particularly those that will benefit the whole church under the particular circumstances. We should also not forbid or discourage any spiritual gift, as long as it is used to bless everyone. We should conduct all spiritual activities in a decent and orderly manner so as to fulfill the supreme objective of glorifying Jesus Christ and edifying his body.

## Title: Revival In A Plain Brown Wrapper

Title: Revival In A Plain Brown Wrapper 
Date: 8/3/25 — 8/18/25
Author: Carlton L. Coon, Sr.
Introduction
Too often, the gift wrapping is more thought out or extravagant than the gift within. Revival, on the other hand, should be wrapped in a plain brown wrapper, with the time and dedication being spent on obtaining the gift rather than its surroundings.
First of All Prayer
“First of all, prayer.” If you want a revival, you’re going to need to pray. Once someone can get the whole church praying in one mind and one accord, only then can we see a revival. The reason conferences like IYC or NAYC “work” is because a group of people got together and began praying and fasting, asking God to do a mighty work.
Prayer is to be expected (e.g., musicians, singers, and service participants): “No pray, no play!”
Even as a church’s pastor, they too should be with the other men praying in the prayer room at 17:30.
Creating A Climate
We should want a revival like Acts 2:47, where God added to the church daily.
God created the ocean for the fish to live in before he made the fish. God created the ground of the earth for the plants and animals to dwell before He created the animals. We must create an atmosphere for revival before we can have revival. We must create a healthy environment, a growth environment, before it becomes necessary.
Swelling is not the same as growth. Swelling is often the result of another church's split and tends to lead to shrinkage.
A crowd and a church are also different. A church is a group of called-out believers, but a crowd is a group of people who want to be entertained. We are called to entertain the presence of God, actively cultivating areas of our lives for Him, not the other way around. The church is not a circus.
People who are busy with a priority of self-satisfaction will not produce a growth climate.
Have Good Church!
A time of good church can be had with any congregation size.
Hook: the current issue
Look: the societal response
Book: what the Bible teaches instead
Took: what can be applied
Plan your service expecting a move of God, then get out of the way and let God work!
Have Good Church, Part 2 – Get the Recipe Right
Too many hands in one dish, each adding its own seasoning, will eventually ruin a dish. Revival is the same way, if too many people are trying their ‘own thing’, then the main course– being Jesus Christ– is going to be tarnished.
Have Good Church, Part 3 – Little Things Mean a Lot
The first 30 seconds of a message are vital; it may only be within the first 10 that the audience decides whether they are or aren’t going to listen.
Don’t re-preach after a guest minister. The church knows you’re the pastor, so don’t let insecurities creep in and try to add to the message. Worst case, a terrible message will leave the congregation grateful for you as their pastor. Best case, the message is excellent and will linger between God and the listener for the coming seasons.
As a pastor, it is essential to get “in the zone” before a church service. It is ill-advised to schedule any sort of pre-service meeting or anything that will take away from the preparation and sensitivity necessary to have good church.
In all churches, there will be winter seasons where the growth is primarily in the roots, but these seasons are just as vital.
If an issue arises mid-service, handle it as discreetly as possible.
Preach out of anointing, not out of emotion.
Tornado Warning — A Real Praise Atmosphere
New converts should never out-praise those of us who have experienced God’s goodness our whole lives.
Pew praise must be learned before platform praise, so as not to become a “praiser” in the sense that praise happens only seldom when one is on the platform.
Effectiveness is more important than preference, whether that be my preference or yours.
You can’t make people praise God, but you can lead them to praise God.
A tornado watch does not mean a tornado has touched down, but it does mean that the atmospheric conditions are just right for a tornado to form. Our churches should have a spiritual tornado watch, meaning that the conditions should be just right for God’s presence to touch down and move when the time is right.
Entertainment vs. Worship Led: There is a time and a place for both. Being worship-led is more ideal because it means that a person is no longer focused or dependent upon the worship leader.
A horse is only as good as its hooves. A car is only as good as its tires. A church is only as good as the prayer life therein. Prayer sets the church in motion; it is essential to check if the low tire pressure light is on in your church’s prayer life.
Beyond Praise to Worship
Praise vs. Worship: a service should start with praise, the often loud and busy act of people giving thanks to God for what He has done, but it should end in worship, a more still and quiet act of people talking one-on-one with God, no matter how many others are in the room.
Silence is okay; dead air is not dead when God is in the room.
Practical Steps in Creating the Atmospheric Conditions for Praise
What sound is exhibited in the auditoriums or meeting rooms before a church service? Is it the sound of prayers to God? Is it positivity, or at least a mindset with a positive trajectory?
It is essential to build while preaching. When a preacher starts immediately on the higher end of preaching, they do so in disregard of the congregation’s daily life. While the preacher likely spends the last hour (and hopefully longer) in prayer and devotion, it is unlikely the congregation is aware of the coming message.
Psalm 150:6 → “If you are breathing here today, join us in some way to praise the LORD.”
Adrenaline or Commitment-Based?
Adrenaline and excitement don't last; commitment does. Live, preach, and teach commitment.
While it may be true that at the fall of Jericho's walls adrenaline was high, it was the mundane that got the Israelites to that place.
Prayer “without ceasing” Was and is a labor of dedication rather than excitement. Adrenaline does not fill the schedule on the prayer chain - commitment does.
No amount of planning, programming, concerts, or events can replicate what God does.
Just as factories do, it is essential to “shut down” and focus on the cleaning or retooling; Pastor Morrell Cornwell (Wichita, Kansas) has closed down the choir for several months each year so that each member can focus on prayer and evangelism (i.e., other spiritual avenues and disciplines).
Systems for Consistent Growth
Purpose
Passion
Process
We see systems just about anywhere in life: the cycle of day and night, the seasons, a generation preceding another generation, as well as one thereafter, etc. In Leviticus 6:8-13, we see a system ordered by God to keep a consistent fire upon the altar; this can only be achieved with a system of ministry.
Only doing things on a whim grows neither people nor churches.
You Gotta Get ‘em in the House
Just as a person needs a doctor to first check their vitals before making a diagnosis, we need to evaluate the church before diagnosing its current state.
Consistent revival is the result of doing the right things repetitively.
Lifestyle Evangelism
A “Philip-Ethiopian eunuch” experience is rare; we need to be prepared to pour into a person consistently before they receive Jesus.
“Go ye into all the world…” or it could be translated, “As ye are going into the world…”
Evangelism should not be a program of the church; it is the behavior of a church.
When the queen of Sheba came to visit Solomon, she did so on a normal day, and even on a normal day, she was impressed.
“Sunday morning for evangelism, Sunday evening for edification, and midweek for equipping.”
Make Room for the Holy Ghost
 The Holy Ghost is not a spectator,  but rather is active–  and should be active throughout every believer's walk with God.
Acts 8:29
Acts 10:19
Acts 11:12
Acts 16:7
Pastoral Prayer: “Jesus, you have a plan for this service, don't let my plan get in the way of your plan.”
 We confuse activity for anointing and volume for victory.
“Strike while the iron is hot!”
Be wary of the disease known as platformitis.
Don’t Forget the Kids
“Feed my lambs… feed my sheep… feed my lambs” (John 21:15-17). Jesus emphasized feeding children (lambs) as well as the older generations (sheep). Jesus was and is serious about the little lambs.
Be a Jehosheba and save a Joash from Athaliah.
Get kids involved, both the kids who are regulars and the ones whose parents aren’t in church (we cannot discriminate; Jesus never did).
Embrace the “So That” Principle
 Accomplishing one task without having the “next task” in mind will result in limited accomplishment.
 Every word or act of God is part of a process for moving toward his eternal objective.
 The opposite of a “so that" mentality is an “end-all” mentality.
Do “It” Again
 One’s ministry should not look like the Sarah Winchester mansion.
 In the book of Malachi, one reads about Israel becoming weary in worship. They grew tired of the repetitiveness of it all,  but God wanted the repetition of worship. Oftentimes, the “boring”  is what works and what God wants.
 After centuries of time, each morning God may say, ‘Do it again’ to the sun, and every evening say, ‘Do it again’ to the moon.
A message of “Where did he come up with that?” isn’t always beneficial because sometimes it leaves a person without the message of salvation.
There is something to be said for deciding what one will do well, and then repeating, improving, and repeating…
We should not be a Mars Hill adherent (the least effective message of Paul) seeking a new thing.
Mrs. Wilson’s Secret
God has a specific vision for each person, do you?
Eyes that look are common, while seeing eyes are rare.
“‘If you treat an individual as he is, he will stay that way, but if you treat him as if he were what he could be, he will become what he could be.’”
Teach People to Apply God’s Word!
Teaching leads to commitment, which leads to revival.
Teaching obtains, retains, and sustains saints.
The preachers we see at IYC, NAYC, or camp meetings don’t preach like that at their church every service.
Effective teaching…
is repetitious
leads to application
makes the Bible relevant to life
is focused
begins where people are
involves mental engagement between the teacher and students
“‘Teaching is telling, preaching is yelling.’” Sometimes telling is more effective, but not always.
Topical, expositional, and biographical.
“‘A text outside of context is just a pretext.’” We must give context to our text.
Value People - Each Person is Significant
Jesus loved Peter and John the same, but he did not treat them the same. With Peter, Jesus could be more blunt than He could with John the Beloved.
Don’t gossip when you should be shepherding.
Don’t Sweat the Small Stuff!
If you’re a pastor, get among the sheep; if you’re an evangelist, get among the sinners.

## Elijah

Elijah
Elijah’s name ['ēlîyâ (ay-lee-yaw')] means “My God is Jehovah,” which defines his mission in life, to make sure everyone knows the supreme power of God.

1 Kings 17
No one dared to confront King Ahab and his belief of Baal because many of the other nations and their people believed that the worship of Baal brought forth rain. Interestingly, Elijah tells Ahab that the LORD, God of Israel, will withhold the rain from the land of Israel and the surrounding areas when the god which Ahab serves is supposedly the god of storms.
During this famine, Elijah was fed by ravens, which were a kind of scavenger and known to not leave food lying around.
In the heart of the Baal worship, in a Phonecian town of Zarephath, there was a widow woman who remained faithful to Jehovah. As a result, her and her son were fed with plenty during the famine, and the son was brought back to life.
1 Kings 18
Obadiah, while a trusted member of Ahab’s court (given that he was the governor of the house), remained faithful to Jehovah. Obadiah hid 100 of the LORD’s prophets, fifty per cave, and fed them with bread and water. When Elijah meets Obadiah, the prophet instructs him to tell Ahab that Elijah is here.
Baalim is the plural of the word Baal, which indicates that there could have been many gods that fell under the same understanding of Baal, and that King Ahab and others could use this to unite the kingdom of Israel.
Mount Carmel was considered a location of reverence and believed to be a place favored by Baal, so when Elijah confronts the prophets of Baal, it becomes even more embarrassing when their god doesn’t answer, no matter how many hours they spent calling on him.
It was a prominent belief that the gods slept at night, but when Elijah mocks Baal, he implies that the god is still asleep during noon.
Elijah waits until nightfall so that no one could claim the sun started the fire rather than God.
Elijah’s short prayer, especially when compared to the 450 prophets of Baal who called upon their god for 12 hours, was all it took for God to step onto the scene.
1 Kings 19
There is a strong parallel between Elijah and Moses. Both men confronted pagan rulers, killed those who worshipped false gods, fled into the wilderness to save their life, and both would be seen with Christ on the Mount of Transfiguration (Mark 9:1-7).
The fire, wind, thunder, and earthquakes were thought to be the method by which gods’, and God’s, voice rang out. This was true for God, but beyond that, God’s voice could be heard as a still small voice, being present no matter the circumstance or situation.
1 Kings 21
Elijah confronts Ahab for not respecting the law of property when the king takes control over Naboth’s vineyard after his wife has Naboth killed. Ahab understood the law, and somewhat respected it, but Jezabel, the Phoenician princess, sees them as something that can be exploited.
2 Chronicles 21:12-15
These verses include Elijah’s letter to Jehoram, prophesying his demise for not following in the ways of his father Jehoshaphat. Jehoram had fallen in love with the things of Ahab, including his daughter whom Jehoram took to wife.
2 Kings 1
Ahaziah sends men to seek counsel from Baal-zebub when he becomes bedridden. Elijah confronts the men of Israel asking if there is not a God in Israel. Elijah tells the men to tell their king that he will die in that bed for seeking advice from false gods. Ahaziah only served as a king for two years
Elijah called fire down from heaven to consume his enemies, 200 men in total until the angel of the LORD gave him the okay.
2 Kings 2
Elijah kept telling Elisha to stay, first just before traveling to Beth-el, then Jericho, and then again before Jordan. But Elisha said he would not leave Elijah’s side (very similar to Ruth and Naomi).
Elijah is taken up into Heaven and lays his mantle onto Elisha.

Elisha
Elisha’s name ['ĕlîšāʿ (el-ee-shaw')] means “God is salvation.”

1 Kings 19
[1Ki 19:15-17 KJV] 15 And the LORD said unto him, Go, return on thy way to the wilderness of Damascus: and when thou comest, anoint Hazael [to be] king over Syria: 16 And Jehu the son of Nimshi shalt thou anoint [to be] king over Israel: and Elisha the son of Shaphat of Abelmeholah shalt thou anoint [to be] prophet in thy room. 17 And it shall come to pass, [that] him that escapeth the sword of Hazael shall Jehu slay: and him that escapeth from the sword of Jehu shall Elisha slay.
Abelmeholah: the city which Elisha was born
Shaphat: the father of Elisha
[1Ki 19:19-21 KJV] 19 So he departed thence, and found Elisha the son of Shaphat, who [was] plowing [with] twelve yoke [of oxen] before him, and he with the twelfth: and Elijah passed by him, and cast his mantle upon him. 20 And he left the oxen, and ran after Elijah, and said, Let me, I pray thee, kiss my father and my mother, and [then] I will follow thee. And he said unto him, Go back again: for what have I done to thee? 21 And he returned back from him, and took a yoke of oxen, and slew them, and boiled their flesh with the instruments of the oxen, and gave unto the people, and they did eat. Then he arose, and went after Elijah, and ministered unto him.
Elijah allowed Elisha to tell his parents goodbye, which was a different reaction than that of the Christ in Luke 9 when a man asks to bury his father. This points to the understanding that the man from the New Testament wasn’t just asking to bury his recently passed father, but instead wanted to wait on his father to die so he could claim his inheritance. As we will see, Elisha wasn’t concerned with his inheritance, instead, he dedicated himself to his calling.
2 Kings 2
Elijah kept telling Elisha to stay, first just before traveling to Beth-el, then Jericho, and then again before Jordan. But Elisha said he would not leave Elijah’s side (very similar to Ruth and Naomi).
Elisha asks Elijah for a double portion of his spirit just before Elijah is taken up into Heaven.
After Elisha takes up the mantle of Elijah, he smites the river Jordan and it parts. Talk about faith! Not only in God, but faith in other men of God.
These men wanted to go and find the body of Elijah, and Elisha told them no, but because they continued asking, he sent them on the wild goose chase they so desired.
Elisha heals the water and land of Jericho. Elisha casts salt into the naught water and barren land and both are healed.
While the men called for Elijah to “come down,” these children called for Elisha to “go up,” and both met their demise by fire and bears.
2 Kings 3
The three kings of Israel, Judah, and Edom, seek council with Elisha, who tells them he only does so for Jehosaphat’s being there (Jehosaphat was ⅛ of the kings of Judah that did right in the eyes of God). Elisha tells these men that God will deliver the Moabites into their hands. The battle is in favor of these kings until Moabite King Mesha sacrifices his eldest son for a burnt offering. Because of Israel’s apostasy, the forsaking of their belief and faith in Jehovah, He gives the battle to the Moabites, which resulted in the artifact called the Moabite Stone (which is a boasting from Mesha and his sacrifice of his son to Chemosh).
2 Kings 4
Elisha multiplies the oil of the poor woman. Elisha tells the woman to pour her pot of oil into all the containers found in her home so that she can then sell it all and pay off her husband’s debtors and free her sons from being bondservants.
Just as Elisha was a servant to Elijah, Gehazi was a servant to Elisha. Both servants were “sons of the prophets,” not referring to lineage but rather occupation.
Elisha revives the Shunammite woman’s son.
Elisha restores the pottage so that it could be eaten again. These men had unknowingly put gourds from a wild vine that were deemed poisonous into pottage. Biblical scholars and botanists identify them as Citrullus colocynthis (commonly known as the colocynth, bitter apple, or vine of Sodom). After Elisha adds meal, or flour, into the pot, the people were able to safely eat the pottage, completely neutralizing the poisonous food.
2 Kings 5
Elisha heals Namman the leper. Elisha tells Namman to dip himself seven times in the Jordan river and that he would then be cured of his leprosy. Namman had much doubt, wondering why the rivers named Arbana and Pharpar of Damascus, mightier rivers than Jordan, could not have healed him. To his servant’s advice, he did as the prophet said and was cleaned.
Elisha’s servant, Gehazi, asks for a reward from Namman after Elisha had denied taking anything in return for his miracle. As a result, Elisha curses Gehazi with the leprosy of Namman.
2 Kings 6
Elisha’s mercy toward the Syrians when the king of Israel asks if they should kill them ends up endangering Elisha’s life from Israel’s king. Elisha still knew that this decision was of the LORD when he says in verse 33, “Behold, this evil is of the LORD,” while sitting in the protection of the elders.
2 Kings 8
Elisha prophesies to Hazael that he should be king over Syria. Hazael then goes and kills Ben-hadad, the current Syrian king who is very sick.
2 Kings 9
Elisha tells one of the children of the prophets to anoint Jehu as king of Israel. Jehu then killed Joram, king of Israel, and Ahaziah, king of Judah. Next, Jehu commanded that Jezebel be thrown down. She fell at such a velocity that her blood sprinkled the walls. After being walked over by a horse, the only thing they were able to bury (and they buried her out of respect that she was a king’s daughter) was her skull, feet, and palms.
2 Kings 13
Elisha prophesies to Joash before the prophet dies. Elisha prophesies that Joash will triumph over Syria, but only thrice because he only smote the ground thrice instead of awaiting further instructions. Elisha then dies and is buried.
After dying, a man is thrown into the sepulchre of Elisha, and as he touches the bones of the prophet, he comes back to life. This double portion prophet performed miracles after death.

## Jonah

Jonah
Jonah’s name [yônâ (yo-naw')] means “dove.”
In the Bible, the dove is a rich symbol representing new beginnings, the Holy Spirit, peace, and purity.

2 Kings 14
[2Ki 14:23-25 KJV] 23 In the fifteenth year of Amaziah the son of Joash king of Judah Jeroboam the son of Joash king of Israel began to reign in Samaria, [and reigned] forty and one years. 24 And he did [that which was] evil in the sight of the LORD: he departed not from all the sins of Jeroboam the son of Nebat, who made Israel to sin. 25 He restored the coast of Israel from the entering of Hamath unto the sea of the plain, according to the word of the LORD God of Israel, which he spake by the hand of his servant Jonah, the son of Amittai, the prophet, which [was] of Gathhepher.
Jonah is first mentioned in the passage of scripture, during the reign of Amaziah of Judah and Jeroboam II of Israel. He is the son of Amittai from the land of Gathhepher.
Jonah 1
Instead of going to Nineveh, Jonah flees to Tarshish.
A storm arises and all the men on the ship fear for their lives, praying to their gods, except Jonah who was fast asleep.
Jonah offers to be thrown overboard so the men throw him into the water, and the strom ceases. The men then grow to fear the LORD, God of Israel, and make sacrifices and vows unto Him.
The LORD prepared a great fish to swallow Jonah and keep him for three days and nights.
Jonah 2
Jonah prayed from the belly of the great fish, describing his circumstances as “the belly of hell.”
The great fish vomited Jonah on the shore of dry land.
Jonah 3
Nineveh was a three day journey from the start to the end of the city.
All the people repented of their evil, while fasting from both food and water (as did all the beasts of the land), hoping the LORD too would turn away from the evil He intended for them.
Jonah 4
Jonah becomes displeased with the mercy of God. The same mercy that brought Jonah out of the belly of the great fish saved the people of Nineveh, sixscore thousand people.
Jonah is angry and wishes the LORD would kill him. Earlier, Jonah cried from the belly of hell, that the LORD would hear his affliction, and now He wishes for the LORD to afflict him.
God sets up a gourd to overshadow Jonah. The LORD then sends a worm to destroy the gourd, and Jonah grows angry for the gourd. God tells Jonah that he did not labor or grow the gourd, but is troubled by its demise, and yet Jonah does not feel the same towards the people of Nineveh.

## 2025 Leadership Training: The Blindspots in Leadership
**Source:** Google Doc

---

2025 Leadership Training: The Blindspots in Leadership
~ Brother Carlton L. Coon Sr. (8/9/25)
Single Perspective: having the viewpoint of only oneself, which disregards others’ views.
A person with this mindset looks for a yes man, someone who will agree with them regardless of the circumstances. We need someone who will shut us down and tell us when our ideas are stupid.
Nehemiah
What had taken 70 years, Nehemiah did in 52 days by placing people to build the part of the wall closest to each worker’s household.
David
Due to his narrow perspective, David lost his influence over his kingdom because he didn’t step in when he should have – Tamar, Absalom, etc. People are always watching; we are never “off.”
Gideon
God chose this man, but Gideon did not obey God and led his people into idolatry.
Insecurity
Moses
He was insecure about his speech and insisted his brother would make a better leader. While Aaron may have spoken well, he was an awful leader of God’s people.
Lack of Knowledge
He who knows not and knows not that he knows not is a fool; shun him.
He who knows not and knows that he knows not is a student; teach him.
He who knows and knows not that he knows is asleep; wake him.
He who knows and knows that he knows not is wise, follow him.
Every ministry leader is constantly learning new things; “Leaders are readers.”
Devaluing People
Leaders who value their people serve their people with the intent of helping them grow to the next level of Christian life.
Leaders who value their people serve their people; leaders who devalue their people want to be served by their people.
Leaders who value their people empower them; leaders who devalue their people control them.
Leaders who value their people motivate them; leaders who devalue their people manipulate them.

Student Leadership Pre-Meeting Notes: Character
~ Brother Tyler Green (11/8/25)
Out of John Maxwell’s three pillars of trust (competence, character, and connection), character is the most important in leadership.
It does not matter how talented you are or how many people like you; if you don’t have good character, you can’t be a good leader.
Character is a lot like the foundation of a building; everything in life is built on it. While we can’t always see the foundation, it is necessary; when it begins to crack, the whole building is affected.
“Talent will open the door for you, but character will keep you in the room.”
If Joseph disobeyed God and lay with Potiphar's wife, there was a chance he would have never been caught and thrown in jail. Joseph does not do this and demonstrates good character. As a result, he had the opportunity to prepare the land for the famine that was to come, reconcile with his family — his brothers and father, who were ever so dear to him — and receive his father's blessing.
We cannot control every situation or outcome, but we can always control our character.
Student Leadership Meeting Notes: Influence
~ Brother Tyler Green (11/8/25)
“Let no one despise your youth, but be an example to the believers in speech, in conduct, in love, in spirit, in faith, and in purity.” 1 Timothy 4:12 MEV
Leadership is never about a title, it’s about influence.
What influences us is trust; when we trust someone, or lack trust in people, that will determine our response.
A dictator has a high-level position, but lacks influence.
We can’t have maximum influence if we are only part-time Christians, or part-time leaders.
If we aren’t influencing people to become closer with God, then we are probably influencing people to move farther from Him.
There are thermometer people and then there are thermostat people; there are people that feel change and then there are people that make change.
Influence is like Axe body spray, even though we can’t always see it when someone has it, it still creates an impact.
To be an influencer, 1) we must care about people and 2) serve people.
Don’t pretend to care about people, that won’t last.
“He who is greatest among you shall be your servant. For he who exalts himself will be humbled, and he who humbles himself will be exalted.” Matthew 23:11-12 MEV
